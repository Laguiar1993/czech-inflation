# R30 results: the excise calendar is right about January spirits and cannot time the tobacco round; the declared candidate is not promoted

20 September 2026, Claude. **One declared run on the recorded release-eve backtest, five variants, none promoted.** Published excise decisions for cigarettes and spirits, entered as statutory steps into the alcohol-and-tobacco block, lower the block's error over the 90 prints (0.919 → 0.902) and raise it from 2024 (0.846 → 0.874) and in the flash era (0.789 → 0.831): the declared timing rule for the cigarette round is wrong in a way no pre-sample calibration fixes, and the statutory size overstates the spring round because a third to a half of each increase arrives in the August–September manufacturer round instead. The January spirits steps are right-signed at every one of the four Januaries they enter and roughly right-sized in 2024 and 2025. Read before the scores, the gate table said all of this.

Two facts follow. The sourced cigarette steps pass through at 50–80% in the spring window (the manufacturer round in August–September — 1.5 to 3.6% of the class in every year 2021–2025 — carries the rest); the reconstructed 2020–21 rows exceed 1 and are the rows one should trust least. And the month is not predictable: with a 1 January change the class rose in February–March from 2022 (Feb 1.4–1.9, Mar 0.8–2.3, Apr ≤ 0.6), in March–April in 2016–2018 (Feb ≤ 0.4), and with the 1 February 2024 change in March–April; the declared "one third in each of the three months after the effective month" is right on the middle month only. The pre-sample years cannot calibrate the timing because it moved.

## What the calendar does get right, and the declaration that follows

The Januaries. Block error (forecast minus print) at the Januaries with a spirits step: 2020 −2.15 → −1.17, 2024 −1.13 → −0.54, 2025 −1.01 → −0.48, 2026 −1.55 → −1.43; the three Januaries without a step move by 0.1–0.2 the wrong way (the past-mean subtraction). A diagnostic that keeps the calendar's January steps and leaves the tobacco round to the seasonal mean scores block 0.890 / 0.805 / 0.751 and headline (STRUCT_EVE) .4182 / .2112 / — every sample better than recorded. **It is a diagnostic, not a candidate: it was seen on the outcomes.** It is declared here as `ALC_JANUARY_STEPS_R30B` (spirits steps only, θ = 0.45, the sourced rows) for a prospective test from the January 2027 print (the 2027 spirits rate must first be sourced; the MF release covers tobacco to 2027, not spirits), and it can be judged on the historical sample only by an independent review that treats the selection as what it is.

January 2026 is the other thing the gate turned up: the alcohol class rose 10.7% in that month, 3.4 points more than a normal January and four times the spirits step. No decision in the calendar explains it; something else repriced alcoholic beverages in January 2026, and the nowcast (recorded block error −1.55) did not know it either. That is worth a source before the next January.

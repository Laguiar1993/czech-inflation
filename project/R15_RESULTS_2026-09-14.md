# Czech CPI path research — trend, residuals and extra predictors

The R15 experiment is complete. The strongest new path is the **fast state-space core trend**. It improves full-sample headline and underlying-core accuracy against the existing bridge. The richer elastic nets and residual forest do not improve on it consistently. The existing **current-core path** remains stronger in the recent period. These results support keeping two understandable research paths on the screen; they do not support replacing them with a large optimized ensemble.

There is a useful but fragile CNB-relative result: FAST has lower full-sample squared error than CNB, but higher average absolute error and more material losses than gains. Its squared-error advantage depends on the February 2022 forecast round. It has not demonstrated reliable advance prediction of fresh underlying-core turns.

[Open CNB Rounds Replayed — R15](output/research_r15/evaluation/cnb_rounds_replayed_r15.html). The original local Claude-style layout is retained, with report/cutoff clocks, a round selector, all fourteen model choices, monthly paths, quarterly averages and equal-support score panels. Default visible models are FAST, current core and stable pipeline; this changes presentation only, not selection or scores. The latest frozen origin is **July 2026**, not a new September live forecast.

## What was built and why

The headline path still begins at the independent HARD_BASE h0 nowcast. For h1 through h12, R15 replaces only the core component of STABLE_PIPELINE_R14B. Its food, fuel, administered prices, alcohol/tobacco, accounting wedge and aggregation weights remain fixed. That makes differences attributable to the core experiment. Existing model outputs and operating entry points were preserved.

The new core forecast has three parts:

1. **Persistent trend:** an estimate of the current underlying monthly pace of core inflation. A Kalman filter updates it as observations arrive; it is not forced back to a 2% headline target.
2. **Temporary pressure:** a separate component which decays by 20% each month. This can represent an unusually strong or weak observation that should not be extrapolated indefinitely.
3. **Destination-month seasonality:** January and other seasonal effects estimated using only the history available at that simulated origin.

In log-monthly percentage-point units, the future forecast is `trend + 0.8^(h+1) × temporary pressure + seasonal effect`. The last observed core month is t−1, so the h1 forecast is two transitions ahead. Each historical origin stores its own state and seasonal factors permanently. The decomposition is economically interpretable, but it is not a causal identification of permanent versus temporary shocks: covariance ratios are fixed assumptions, and simultaneous changes in prices can still be misclassified.

Three fixed filters let the trend update slowly, moderately or quickly. A fourth chooses among them using matured historical errors. The faster filter won the full-sample comparison; the adaptive selector did not automatically discover the retrospective headline winner. It selects on delayed core-band errors, not future headline results, and long-horizon feedback arrives slowly.

The learned models forecast a **correction to the moderate filter**, separately for h1–3, h4–6, h7–9 and h10–12. Elastic nets test domestic signals, imported-cost signals, both together, and a wider hard-data panel. A forest predicts the residual with domestic and imported signals together. Two fixed 50/50 combinations were declared before the full run: linear+forest and wide elastic net+moderate state. Their weights were not optimized after observing results.

This addresses the level-forest extrapolation problem: the moving state baseline can shift beyond historical inflation levels, while the forest learns a smaller departure from that baseline. It does not make trees capable of extrapolating beyond their residual support, or make the residual forecast automatically good.

The state-space motivation is related to [Stock and Watson, Core Inflation and Trend Inflation](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21282). R15 is a small fixed-variance approximation, not their multivariate stochastic-volatility estimator. The nonlinear comparison is informed by [CNB WP9/2026](https://www.cnb.cz/export/sites/cnb/en/economic-research/.galleries/research_publications/cnb_wp/cnbwp_2026_09.pdf); this experiment is not a replication of that paper.

## Inputs and chronological selection

No professional inflation expectations, household inflation expectations, CNB forecasts or confidence/sentiment surveys enter these R15 models. CNB forecasts enter only the evaluator.

| Predictor group | Main signals | Candidate columns including destination seasonality |
|---|---|---:|
| Own + domestic | Core pace/acceleration and states; broad services inflation/change; unemployment/change; industrial-production growth; unit-labour-cost growth | 13 |
| Own + imported | Core states; broad goods inflation/change; EURCZK changes; German HICP; import prices; producer prices; producer-price real exchange rate; metals | 23 |
| Own + both | Combined domestic and imported signals | 29 |
| Wide hard-data set | Own states, broad services/goods, FX and 32 hard-data A6 rows, including commodity and forward-price signals | 45 |

These broad services/goods series are predictors, not an exact decomposition of the CNB core basket. The shorter detailed hotels, restaurants and other category histories were not silently spliced into a longer training sample. Revised Rushin/LUCI histories were excluded from this experiment's wide set. Exact column names are in [feature_groups.json](output/research_r15/feature_groups.json), and every consumed value is in [features_by_origin.csv](output/research_r15/features_by_origin.csv).

Two important source details were settled before the full fit. First, the consumed panel's quarterly nominal ULC variable is an NSA index level, not the annual growth rate in a different panel; the loader asserts that source identity. Second, the inherited paper panel added an extra twelve-row shift to year-one gas/Brent futures. R15 reconstructs the current available user-selected forward quotes from the frozen raw inputs without that extra delay, retaining an explicit [availability ledger](output/research_r15/futures_availability.csv). This does not modify the original paper panel or claim exact Refinitiv/constant-maturity replication.

Each learner uses at most the last 120 eligible historical origins, with at least 48 required. A target band is eligible only after every target month has been released and precedes the current origin. Imputation and scaling use its training rows only. Elastic nets compare six predeclared penalty configurations; forests compare two leaf sizes with 150 trees and seed 42. Selection uses the last 36 common matured candidate forecasts, with 24 required before overriding the fixed default. All 2,160 scored-origin selection decisions had 36 eligible validation rows.

Thus chronological parameter selection was actually performed. A large unrestricted parameter search was not. The weak wide-model results provide no reason to assume that dramatically increasing search volume would fix this sample's signal-to-noise problem.

Recent *target-month* scores are also exported. That different frame includes forecasts issued before 2024: FAST h12 RMSE is 1.788, versus stable pipeline 1.686 and current core 2.619. There is no model that wins every honest frame.

Why did the extra data not help? Since 2024, the strongest elastic-net penalty is selected in 105–109 of 124 origin/band fits per feature group. Every such fit has exactly zero slopes and zero residual intercept: it returns the moderate state baseline. Independent refits and the optimization conditions confirm that this is real shrinkage, not a coding fallback. In earlier turbulent periods, the wide model permits much larger extrapolations and its h12 score is substantially worse. More inputs and the ability to extrapolate are not sufficient for better forecasts.

The [linear driver report](output/research_r15/interpretation/linear_drivers.csv) exactly decomposes 1,440 elastic-net predictions into the intercept and transformed-input contributions. These are fitted contributions, not causal effects; their reconstruction error is below 9e−16 monthly log percentage points.

## Comparison with CNB: large misses versus ordinary accuracy

CNB is scored at two separately retained clocks: the latest existing model snapshot before the report day, and the latest snapshot through the CNB cutoff day. Both use the same 66 report/quarter pairs, from 18 scoreable reports and 18 distinct realised quarters. Overlapping quarters are dependent observations, not 66 independent trials. The replay contains 19 report rounds, including the final unscored round.

| Model / clock | Quarterly MAE | Quarterly RMSE | Material gains / losses versus CNB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CNB | **1.061** | 2.295 | — |
| Independent bridge, report clock | 1.528 | 2.362 | 12 / 35 |
| Current core, report clock | 1.432 | 2.339 | 17 / 29 |
| **FAST, report clock** | 1.260 | **1.987** | **15 / 32** |
| FAST, cutoff clock | 1.335 | 2.129 | 13 / 31 |

A material gain requires at least **0.15pp less absolute error** than CNB; a material loss requires at least 0.15pp more. Overshooting in the correct direction can still lose. FAST's better RMSE indicates improvement on some very large misses, while its worse MAE and gain/loss record show that the advantage is not broad or frequent.

The concentration matters. FAST's total report-clock squared-error advantage is 87.26 squared percentage points across the 66 comparisons; the **February 2022 report alone contributes 139.65**. In a post-run diagnostic which removes each report in turn, the RMSE advantage survives 17 of 18 omissions. Removing February 2022 reverses it: FAST 1.739 versus CNB 1.476 on the remaining 62 pairs. The cutoff-clock result also reverses. This diagnostic changes neither the primary full sample nor any model selection; it shows dependence on one episode. See [all leave-one-report-out results](work/research_r15_design/cnb_leave_one_report_out.csv).

In February 2022 the model projected more inflation than CNB and was much closer on several later quarters. This is evidence about the forecast difference, not evidence that it foresaw the subsequent war or every component of that shock. A plausible higher baseline can help during a surprise without having predicted the event causing it.

For reports from 2024 onward, the 34 common comparisons give:

| Model | Report-clock MAE | Report-clock RMSE | Cutoff-clock MAE | Cutoff-clock RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CNB | 0.287 | **0.372** | 0.287 | **0.372** |
| **Current core** | **0.275** | 0.402 | **0.278** | 0.395 |
| FAST | 0.346 | 0.473 | 0.384 | 0.504 |

Current core's small recent MAE edge is encouraging, but its material record is 8 gains/9 losses at report clock and 8/10 at cutoff. It is not evidence of a frequent, large disagreement advantage. Numerical agreement with CNB is useful context, not the training objective or proof of economic correctness.

## Direction, fresh turns and stability

At h12, among 66 actual headline moves larger than 0.25pp from the origin's last known annual rate, FAST gets the material direction right in **43/66** cases versus bridge **37/66**. Opposite material calls fall from **19 to 10**. The forest gets 46/66 directions right, but has worse magnitude accuracy than FAST. At h3 and h6, FAST's direction scores do not improve on the bridge. Headline annual direction can reflect known base effects; it is not a test of a fresh turn in underlying pressure.

The separate underlying-core test removes each origin's saved seasonal pattern, averages h1–3/4–6/7–9/10–12, and annualizes the log pace. A peak or trough requires two opposite adjacent changes of at least 0.5pp annualized at the middle two bands. There are 159 common origin/band opportunities with 77 actual turns, including overlapping observations.

| Model | Predicted turns | Exact-band hits | False turns |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bridge / pipeline | 38 | 11 | 27 |
| Current core | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| FAST / moderate state | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Adaptive state | 4 | 0 | 4 |
| Elastic net: both | 47 | 8 | 39 |
| Residual forest | 20 | 3 | 17 |

A fixed state path has a constant expected trend plus a positively decaying temporary component. Its seasonally adjusted forecast is monotonic toward that trend, so it cannot generate an internal peak followed by a reversal. This is an architectural limitation, not a surprise result or a software defect. The learned corrections can generate turns, but these calls are poor in this test. **R15 has not solved the goal of anticipating underlying inflation turns several quarters ahead.** Nor can this table establish superiority over CNB's core-turn forecasts, which were not supplied.

The exact-boundary rule is inclusive (at least 0.5), as in the explicit pre-full-run addition. The immutable declaration's word “exceed” was imprecise; no actual or predicted change lies within 1e−10 of exactly 0.5, so the distinction changes no recorded result.

Matched-target forecast revisions are smaller than for the pipeline: FAST mean absolute revision is 0.646pp versus 0.877pp, with maximum revisions 4.235 versus 11.112pp. These use 1,035 matched consecutive-origin/target pairs per model, including future targets with no realised outcome yet. The reviewer caught and corrected an initial evaluation filter which unnecessarily required those outcomes. Revision magnitude measures stability, not whether the revisions were justified by news.

The twelve-month moving-block bootstrap remains descriptive. FAST's paired full-sample squared-loss intervals versus pipeline are below zero at h3/h9/h12, but span zero at h6. There is no multiple-model search correction or untouched holdout; this is not a declaration of statistically established dominance.

## What I would use and improve next

Keep the working model map small:

- **Next release:** existing independent HARD_BASE, with its established HALF/FULL comparisons. R15 changes neither nowcast forecasts nor their survey comparison.
- **Path reference:** retain the independent bridge/stable pipeline as the reproducible benchmark.
- **Two path research lines to inspect:** current core for its recent performance, FAST for more responsive trend adaptation and stronger full-history performance. Show their disagreement; do not silently choose a winner using the eventual outcome.
- **Residual forest:** a diagnostic challenger because of h12 headline direction and full-sample improvement on the bridge. It has not earned automatic blending into the main path.
- **Domestic/imported/wide elastic nets and the two combinations:** retain the results and code, without promotion. Their negative results are useful evidence about the current features and target design.

The next substantive design should let the **trend itself change direction**. I would test a small damped-slope state model and a separately estimated services/goods transmission model. Services pressure and imported-goods pressure should have distinct persistence and lag structures; merely placing broad proxies in one residual regression did not establish those mechanisms. Start with a few prespecified lag shapes or a hierarchical shrinkage model, and compare against FAST/current core on this same scoreboard before increasing complexity. Any new blend weights require their own chronological selection, not a hindsight mixture of today's winners.

For shocks, separate the baseline from explicit energy/FX/policy scenarios and subsequent news revisions. That would make a deviation from CNB explainable: stronger domestic persistence, different import pass-through, or a different energy assumption. The central forecast should not be judged by whether it guessed an unforeseeable war, but its assumptions, transmission, response to new information and errors on the same information set still need scrutiny. No shock periods were removed from this experiment's main scoreboards.

Policy start/expiry accounting, basket exposure and recorded input vintages remain high-priority implementation work. They were deliberately held fixed here. The independent central path should remain separate from any optional expectations-conditioned comparison and from the rates/trading decision layer.

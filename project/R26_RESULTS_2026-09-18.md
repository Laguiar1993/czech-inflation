# R26 results: the sell-side benchmark loses on levels, and the promotion rule re-labels R24

18 September 2026, Claude. **One declared evaluation, no new candidate, nothing promoted.** The practitioner's benchmark relayed by the user (split the basket, seasonally adjust, AR(1) per block) was built the honest way at all 90 origins and scored against the roster. It loses to the roster in nearly every cell, and the assembled sell-side path is 31–48% worse than FAST. The reason is not the seasonal or the autoregressive structure: it is the level each block reverts to after the 2021–23 break. That is also where every gain and every failure of rounds R21 to R25 sat. The promotion rule declared in the specification is applied to the R24 food block: it passes the accuracy conditions and fails the specificity condition, so it is carried forward as a fixed drift, not as an estimator.

[Specification](docs/implementation/R26_BENCHMARK_FAMILY_AND_PROMOTION_RULE_SPEC_2026-09-18.md) (committed as `25d4431` before any code; code as `f77224c` before the run) · Run: `output/research_r26/final` · Tests: `tests/test_benchmarks_r26.py` (9)

## What was done

`models/benchmarks_r26.py` builds four feasible benchmarks per block from realised block rates published by each origin's clock: zero change; the three-year seasonal-naive path; `SA_AR` (trailing window of at most 96 months, centred calendar-month pattern, AR(1) on the adjusted deviation clipped to [0, .95], forecast = window mean + pattern + decaying deviation); and `SA_AR_TARGET` (the same with core's mean fixed at 2% a year). A fifth member, the in-sample best constant, is a ceiling, not a forecast. Every roster block (FAST, current core, gentle slope, the R24 food path) is scored against them on the fixed 969-key primary support, by horizon and era, in cumulative log points of the block's own price level. The benchmarks are then assembled into full paths on the FAST frame (h0, wedge and weights unchanged) and scored like every other path. Reconstruction checks: reassembling FAST from its own blocks reproduces the run to 0.0; the R24 food candidate is reproduced from FAST's block and the drift audit to 9e-16.

## Audit before scores

- All 450 origin-by-block benchmarks estimated (window 96 months for core and administered; 48–96 for food, fuel and alcohol, whose realised series start in 2015).
- AR(1) coefficients: core 0.17–0.81 (0.41 in 2019, 0.65 in 2022, 0.81 from 2024: the surge made core look persistent), fuel 0.29–0.67, food 0–0.35, administered 0–0.22, alcohol and tobacco 0. The practitioner's ARMA has little to work with outside core and fuel.
- Core window mean: 0.09 log points a month at the 2019 origins (about 1% a year), 0.40 at the 2026 origins (about 4.8% a year). This one number is the story of the round.

Reading, block by block:

- **Core.** FAST beats the sell-side benchmark by 25–37% at h3 in every era and by 12% at h12 on the full sample. From 2024 the benchmark is 2.6 times worse at h12 (1.54 against 0.59): its window mean holds the surge and it reverts toward 4–5% a year. Anchoring the mean at 2% (`SA_AR_TARGET`) cuts that error to 1.26, still twice FAST. The one cell the benchmark wins is 2022–23 at h12 (3.34 against 5.09): reversion is right in the unwind, as R25 found. Current core is the best core block from 2024 (0.47) and the worst on the full sample (5.01, behind the benchmark's 4.78).
- **Food.** FAST beats the benchmark by 16–23% at h3 (3% in 2019–21) and the benchmark carries the same window drift as the R14B system: from 2024 at h12 the two are equal (4.03 against 4.13) and both lose to zero change (3.69) and to the R24 path (3.37).
- **Fuel.** The pass-through model beats the benchmark by 10–13%, and zero change is as good as the model: ratios 1.01–1.03 on the full sample and 0.96–0.99 from 2024. Fuel's short-run information is in already observed oil and koruna moves and it is worth about 2% of block RMSE over the sample, nothing since 2024.
- **Administered prices.** Zero change beats FAST from 2024 by 21% at h3 and 38% at h12 (1.15 against 1.85). FAST's block bias from 2024 is +1.5 log points a year, which is the +0.26 pp headline bias in the block attribution; administered prices were flat over 2024–26 (cumulative 0.07 log points a year). The benchmark is four to five times worse from 2024 because its window holds the 2022–23 energy repricing.
- **Alcohol and tobacco.** The benchmark equals FAST (within 3%); zero change is two to three times worse because excise increases are systematic. Nothing to gain here.
- **Ceiling.** The in-sample best constant at h12 is 5.4% a year for core (RMSE 3.79 against FAST 4.28), 5.0% for food (7.45, equal to the R24 path's 7.47), 8.5% for administered prices, 6.2% for alcohol and tobacco, 2.5% for fuel (17.4, equal to FAST). The full sample is dominated by 2021–23; a constant that fits it is wrong from 2024 in every block. This is why every condition in the rule is era-balanced.

**Condition 3, the R24 lesson written down.** Against the grid of fixed annual drifts 1.75–4.75%, the estimator's full-sample h12 squared-loss difference against the best grid constant (4.75%) is +4.6 with interval [−11.2, +26.0]: not distinguishable, and if anything worse. Nine constants, 2.25 to 4.25% a year, pass condition 1 in all twelve era-by-horizon cells; the estimator's own values (2.5–2.8%) sit at the low end of that set. Inside the set the constants trade eras: 2.5% is best from 2024 at h12 (3.40 against 3.37 for the estimator), 4.25% is best on the full sample (7.19), and the era-balanced score (mean over the twelve cells of RMSE relative to the baseline) is flattest at 3.75% (0.933; the estimator 0.946; 2.25% 0.955). The specification names the promoted object as the simplest equivalent member of the grid. I carry forward the food block as **a fixed drift, and keep the long-history median's value (about 2.6% a year) as that number**, because it is the only member of the equivalent set chosen without looking at the outcomes; 3.75% and 4.25% are hindsight picks and are reported as such. The claim that the median *estimator* adds anything is withdrawn.

**Condition 4 failed in one cell** (from 2024 at h6, correlation −0.16; the other three cells +0.41 to +0.64). For a level shift the correlation across origins within an era is the wrong test: the applied correction is nearly constant inside an era, so its correlation with the need is noise, while sign agreement is 0.68–0.75 in every era. The specification is hash-frozen; the amendment for level-type candidates (sign agreement of at least one half, plus agreement of the era means' signs) is declared here and goes into the next specification. Under either reading the estimator claim falls on condition 3.

## What this settles

1. **The roster's edge over the standard sell-side model is the level of each block after a break, not the dynamics.** Where a block's level is right (FAST's core filter in the build-up, zero change for fuel and administered prices, the R24 drift for food), the block is competitive; where it is wrong (a window mean, a window drift, a 2% anchor for core), no autoregressive structure repairs it. Every result from R21 to R25 reads the same way.
2. **Administered prices are the next block.** Zero change from 2024 removes 0.26 pp of headline bias that FAST carries; a candidate that starts from zero change and adds only known regulatory decisions is the obvious form, and the rule gives it a clean test (it must beat `ZERO` in every era, including 2022–23 where zero change is the worst member).
3. **Fuel is done.** The pass-through model is worth 2% of block RMSE over the sample and nothing since 2024; the only remaining information is a futures-curve line, which is a separate conditioned series.
4. **Core from 2024 ranks current core (0.47) ahead of FAST (0.59), the 2% anchor (1.26) and the window mean (1.54); on the full sample the order is FAST, the benchmark, current core.** The phase question stands.
5. **The rule is adopted as declared**, with the condition-4 amendment above for level-type candidates. It is stricter than R24's rule in exactly the way the reviewer asked: R24 passes as a fixed number, not as a method.

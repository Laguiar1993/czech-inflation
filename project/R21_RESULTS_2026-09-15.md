# Czech CPI: R21 model improvement experiments

I tested **four nowcast candidates and eight path candidates**. The best addition is a small correction for persistent core forecast errors. Its gains are modest; the evidence does not justify replacing the live models. The tests also identify where the next substantial work should concentrate: policy-price measurement for the nowcast, and the propagation of distinct domestic and imported inflation pressures for the path.

All fits use the existing 90 monthly origins, February 2019–July 2026. There is no survey or CNB forecast in their inputs or selection objectives. The path scoreboard retains the original 969 scored origin/horizon keys. Inputs remain revised histories with reconstructed publication timing. These are research results on a repeatedly studied sample, not a new untouched holdout or a live record.

Open [the CNB-round interactive replay](output/research_r21/path_anchor/evaluation/cnb_rounds_replayed_r21.html), [the comparison chart](output/research_r21/presentation/model_comparison.png), or [all nowcast release rows](output/research_r21/nowcast/evaluation/releases.csv).

## What was tried and why

**Headline error models.** The existing nowcast predicts separate components, then its FULL correction predicts past errors in core. I tested predicting the first headline release's error directly, using BASE as an offset. One candidate learns a small recent average error; the other uses elastic net with hard economic features, the baseline forecast and previously released headline errors. Both use up to60 past labels, minimum24. The elastic net has fixed regularization, not a search for the best historical setting. Its first24 origins equal BASE and are disclosed separately.

**Simpler residual forests.** A random forest conditional mean and a quantile forest median predict the existing sequential core errors. Both use200 trees, leaf8, all hard features and seed42. These are larger-leaf alternatives to FULL, whose archived forest used leaf3 and a weighted mix of quantiles. Therefore the comparison is not a clean single-factor experiment on quantile weighting. The archived FULL also comes from its earlier runtime. Small differences must not be attributed entirely to a single mathematical change.

**Earlier path learning.** Three experts—FAST, current core and gentle slope—are combined using one weight vector across all12 months. Compare a fixed50/25/25 mix with two learned mixes. One waits for a whole year's outcomes; the other can learn from every released prefix. Both use the same recency-weighted, horizon-balanced loss and shrinkage. Their objective computes compounded monthly CPI exactly. The contrast isolates the delay imposed by waiting for h12; it is not the same objective as the old R17 selector.

**Component error feedback.** Each old h1 core or food forecast is compared with its subsequently published component observation. A shrunk average of up to24 such errors is added to that component's future path and decays with horizon. Core alone, food alone and both are tested. These corrections learn persistent bias; they do not read future errors or change January magnitudes.

**A long-run reference.** After the first batch's results, I declared and tested two additional candidates. The persistent core trend converges gradually toward the CNB's public2% inflation objective, adjusted by a past core/headline spread. Half-lives are12 and24 months. Cycle, seasonal factors, non-core blocks and h0 are preserved. The target is an economic assumption, not the CNB staff forecast, and headline inflation is not forced to2% at h12. The second-stage timing is explicitly recorded in its separate specification.

On the66 origins where headline correction can actually fit, its ENET RMSE is.5583 versus BASE.4640. Including identical warmup forecasts does not explain the failure. The recent-mean correction also fails to improve BASE.

The forest mean reduces average absolute error relative to FULL by about4%, but its RMSE advantage is only.000275pp and is not statistically persuasive. The descriptive six-month block bootstrap interval for that RMSE difference is[-.0123,+.0082]. It gives fewer material big-surprise wins. It is an optional research comparator, not a clear replacement.

**Do not confuse conditional surprise performance with advance detection.** FULL is materially better on9 of23 realised big surprises and worse on2. However, across ALL23 releases where its forecast deviates >=.2pp from consensus, only7 are big surprises and its MAE is.3213 versus consensus.2652 on those same releases. The forest mean also loses to consensus across its24 calls (.3900 versus.3500). No tested alert rule is established as a trading edge.

### Where the largest nowcast mistakes come from

I independently rebuilt all90 FULL forecasts from the old component report's shared non-core legs plus the independent core prediction and correction. Maximum reconstruction error is8.9e-16pp. Component errors plus reconciliation reproduce the headline error.

October2022 and January2023 together account for **48.49% of FULL's squared errors**. October's administered-price error is+2.436pp out of a+2.475pp headline miss. January's administered-price error is-1.008pp out of a-1.162pp headline miss. These are signed contribution errors; other components partly offset them, so they are not additive shares of squared loss.

The result strongly prioritizes correct subsidy/start/expiry measurement and household bill exposure over more core-forest tuning. It does not license inserting historically known CPI movements into an announcement table. A future model must translate genuinely available policy terms into the CPI concept before the print. The national exposure gaps documented in R18/R19 still prevent treating offer prices as a representative household bill forecast.

**Core feedback is worth retaining as a research challenger.** Against FAST, full h6 RMSE improves2.9% and h12 improves1.2%. It also reduces full h3/h6/h12 MAE. Its full-sample RMSE gain at each of those horizons survives omitting any single origin year. Nevertheless the fixed-support12-month-block bootstrap intervals for average squared-loss improvement all include zero. Recent h3 and h6 RMSE worsen slightly relative to FAST; current core and gentle slope remain stronger at recent h12. There is no basis for universal promotion.

**Earlier learning helps the selector, but not enough.** Partial outcomes improve on the otherwise identical complete-outcome selector. Both still lose to the fixed mix and to FAST on full h3/h6/h12. Switching weights in response to errors is not automatically useful when experts share the same missing shock information.

**The anchor has a real tradeoff.** It improves recent h12 against FAST but worsens full-sample RMSE. A nominal reference can make a path more plausible in normal conditions, yet prematurely damp persistent inflation in abnormal ones. Simply pulling a forecast toward2% is not the solution.

### CNB comparison and turning points

On the report-date clock's66 comparable report/quarter pairs, core feedback has MAE1.230 and RMSE1.918, versus FAST1.260/1.987 and CNB1.061/2.295. It has15 material gains and30 material losses versus CNB. Thus lower squared error does not imply typical outperformance.

Removing the February2022 report changes the comparison: core-feedback RMSE1.701 versus CNB1.476, and MAE1.086 versus CNB.704. The full-sample RMSE advantage over CNB is sensitive to that report. In2024+ report pairs core-feedback MAE.359 trails CNB.287 and current core.275. Both report and cutoff clocks, and every report omission, remain in the evidence.

Core feedback improves the sustained-movement diagnostic from FAST's1 hit in8 calls to12 hits in29 calls, with17 false calls. The24-month anchor has12 hits in27 calls and15 false calls. These are overlapping origin opportunities, not independent episodes. Neither achieves a strict interior core turning-point hit under the existing0.5pp exact-band reversal definition. None establishes that we anticipated a turn before CNB. A decaying correction can describe an acceleration/deceleration without locating a peak or trough.

## My recommended direction

1. **Keep the live roster small.** Preserve independent BASE, HALF and FULL comparisons; use their different strengths explicitly. Keep FAST/current core/gentle slope as path references. Retain core feedback in research. Do not route position size automatically from FULL's consensus gap: its all-call record still fails that test.
2. **Nowcast: improve measurement where the error budget points.** Build household energy accounting from tariff components, taxes, policy credits and explicit start/expiry events, with national exposure distinguished from advertised offers. For food, seek information that changes the current month's observation rather than increasingly flexible regressions on the same lagged aggregates. These are proposed priorities, not completed gains in this round.
3. **Path: give domestic and imported pressures different propagation mechanisms.** The next variation should forecast a small system of wage/unit-cost pressure, services inflation, imported-goods costs and energy pass-through, with distributed lags and a slow equilibrium component. Use the existing released macro histories, penalize lag profiles, and assess components before the headline. Earlier attempts to append macro predictors to a forecast-error regression were weak; this proposal changes the transmission structure and must prove its value against those previous failures. A long-run anchor should interact with observable pressure and policy transmission rather than impose unconditional convergence.
4. **Make forecast disagreement interpretable.** Decompose each change into updated observations, a changed trend estimate, pipeline cost pressure and announced policy. Keep commodity/exchange-rate scenarios explicit. Accuracy and sensible conditional economics matter together; neither closeness to the CNB line nor a visually appealing turn proves quality.
5. **Stop selecting on whichever retrospective panel looks best.** Freeze each substantive candidate before its prospective comparison, retain both full and recent scores, ordinary absolute/squared losses, all alerts, and false turns. Today supplies useful rejection evidence and one incremental challenger, not proof of an excellent trading model.

## Reproduction and audit

Specifications: `docs/implementation/R21_RESEARCH_SPEC_2026-09-15.md` and `R21B_ANCHOR_SPEC_2026-09-15.md`. R21B was deliberately declared after viewing first-batch results; the original specification and outputs remain untouched.

```text
python -m tools.research_r21.nowcast --output <new-nowcast-directory>
python -m tools.research_r21.path --output <new-path-directory>
python -m tools.research_r21.evaluate nowcast --root <nowcast-directory>
python -m tools.research_r21.evaluate path --root <path-directory>
python -m tools.research_r21.anchor --output <new-anchor-directory>
python -m tools.research_r21.evaluate_anchor --root <anchor-directory>
```

The anchor runner explicitly consumes the first batch at `output/research_r21/path`; use the frozen packaged version or update that declared input deliberately. Output paths must not already exist. `publish.py` reads the canonical R21 paths and writes the presentation directory once. No live fetch is performed. All model input/output hashes and source versions are included in the delivery manifest.

Independent review: `work/research_r21_review/REVIEW.md`. It reconstructs training labels and scores, checks selected optimizer solutions against a separate global optimizer, and tests future/unpublished-data poisoning. The nowcast first-release table contains dates without intraday stamps; the runner represents them at midnight. Using09:00 instead leaves all frozen release-eve training sets and predictions unchanged. These artifacts make no same-day intraday readiness claim, consistent with the user's instruction that release hours are out of scope.

Final targeted regression run: **24 tests passed**, including nine new R21/R21B tests, prior path-combination tests, current-path accounting tests and independent-nowcast tests. No pre-existing model source was edited by this research batch.

For uncertainty, use `presentation/primary_support_bootstrap.csv` when discussing the fixed969-key scoreboard. The inherited evaluator also exports a separately labelled pairwise-available-support bootstrap with different counts; it is not interchangeable. All intervals are descriptive and unadjusted for repeated research selection.

Sources informing hypotheses: [ECB forecast-combination study, WP1972](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecbwp1972.en.pdf); [CNB official inflation target](https://www.cnb.cz/en/monetary-policy/inflation-target/index.html).

# R15 independent economic and input-timing review

Read-only review of the declared R15 design and frozen input files, 14 September 2026. No estimator or runner files were edited by this reviewer. This note records pre-fit findings, including a withdrawn concern, so it does not silently rewrite the review history.

## Verdict and corrections before fitting

The changing trend plus temporary AR component is a coherent experiment. Forecasting a direct residual around a moving baseline lets total inflation forecasts move beyond the historical response support of a level forest. It does not establish that either the trend or the residual is a separately identified economic shock, or that all learned h12 corrections mean-revert.

1. **Remove the inherited futures lag for this independent experiment.** `tools/paper_replication/build_paper_panel.py` applies `FORWARD_SHIFT={65:12,66:12}` even in its realtime panel. Using that panel unchanged would provide twelve-month-old year-1 prices to the wide model. The parent accepted rebuilding these two columns from frozen raw observations at the same A6 cutoff, omitting that shift and preserving the original panel. T0 is the price level: no extra transformation or publication lag is necessary. Label these user-selected year-1 futures, without claiming exact constant twelve-month maturity.
2. **Avoid duplicated economic inputs in the wide elastic net.** At review time `data/core_trend_residual_r15.py::feature_groups` included both `unemployment` and `a6_11`, and both `cost_N_now` and `a6_N` for N=23,26,45,47,56,62. Exact aliases change effective ridge shrinkage inside an elastic net despite adding no information. The literal declared wide set is own features, broad services/goods and the hard A6 rows. Freeze the intended set before fitting; do not interpret duplicated versions as a clean test of additional information.
3. **Bind ULC concept to the consumed panel.** The actual runner consumes `paper_model_panel_20260912_luci`, whose row17 is the positive Bloomberg nominal ULC index level; its proposed twelve-snapshot log growth is valid. My initial concern about growth-of-a-growth-rate applied to the *different, unconsumed* `paper_model_panel_20260912_exact`, whose row17 is already SA year-on-year percentage growth. That concern is withdrawn as a bug in the actual runner. Assert source and T0 identity from the consumed manifest so swapping panels cannot silently invalidate the formula.

## Verified clocks and horizon interpretation

- `release_eves` explicitly defines A6 row e at the eve of the first release of CPI e+1. Origin t must therefore consume e=t-1. Examples: e=2019-01 maps to 2019-03-10; e=2025-01 maps to 2025-03-04; e=2026-06 maps to 2026-08-04. A6 date-midnight is conservative relative to saved 23:59 origin clocks.
- Comparing adjacent rows in `data/release_calendar_cz_cpi.csv` found no case where detailed CPI t-1 is later than the eve of first CPI t. The declared 36-month core minimum gives the earliest origin January2010, where the actual calendar is already present. The differing pre-2010 fallback clocks do not enter estimable R15 states under that minimum.
- Last observed core is t-1, so the state forecast for t+h needs rho^(h+1). In particular h1 is two transition steps, and h12 is thirteen. Preserving the existing h0 while changing h1..12 is a declared combined nowcast/path design, not a claim that h0 is generated by the new filter.
- Historical baseline forecasts and seasonal vectors must stay attached to their own origin. Full three-month band outcomes, their publication timestamps, and target<t are needed before either fitting or configuration selection. Thirty-six overlapping monthly band errors are not thirty-six independent experiments.
- Three-row averages of A6 realtime snapshots are causal but are not necessarily three distinct source months: irregular releases can repeat a source. IP log growth between snapshots three rows apart is valid but should be called a change in available snapshots. Export source age or make that interpretation explicit.
- `features_at` assumes the declared panel clock matches its supplied origin/as_of. A source-clock assertion is useful protection against later reuse at an earlier custom CNB cutoff. The planned comparison using already-saved earlier snapshots is consistent with this constraint.

## Evaluation recommendations

Retain every episode in full-sample scoring, including policy changes and2022. Show core and cumulative-core errors alongside headline errors, since improved headline predictions can otherwise result from cancellation against unchanged noncore errors. Report fixed-model results as well as selected-model results so slow maturity at long horizons is visible.

For CNB, preserve both declared clocks and common target-quarter support, compare unrounded absolute errors, and show both >=0.15pp gains and deteriorations. A model-CNB deviation in the same direction as realised-CNB error can still overshoot and lose: direction alone is insufficient. Repeated reports for the same realised quarter must remain identifiable.

The proposed annual-inflation sign and adjacent-quarter change scores are useful direction diagnostics, but do not alone measure turning-point timing. Annual inflation can decline through base effects while underlying monthly pressure remains high. For a substantive turn claim, also display predicted and actual core three-month momentum and a frozen diagnostic identifying material local peaks/troughs, reporting timing error, missed turns and false turns. These should not enter parameter selection.

The existing local `R14_PAPERS_AND_BENCHMARKS.md` source notes support the motivation and limits: CNB WP9/2026 discusses forest extrapolation limits; BoE BBIM distinguishes retrospective block decomposition from sequential forecasting and abstracts from publication delays in its forecasting exercise. Neither validates R15's fixed covariance ratios or proves Czech turning-point skill. No new source fetch was needed for this review.

## What changed against R23

R23 concluded that cost gaps add nothing to the FAST core path. The review found it had not tested that: the labour-cost input carried a seasonal worth 44% of the feature's variance, sign bounds switched the imported channel off in 82–98% of fits, and the active term was a penalized intercept whose correction correlated −0.58 with the correction FAST needed.

R23B keeps the harness (FAST baseline, identical saved seasonal layer, 90 origins, 969 scored keys, one training origin per quarter) and repairs the measurement:

- **Labour costs:** real unit labour cost against the same quarter of the three previous years. It carries no seasonal.
- **Imported costs:** six-month import and producer price momentum less core momentum, in place of level gaps, which are late-cycle measures.
- **No intercept and no centring:** zero pressure gives zero correction. A feature whose mean is not zero still carries a level term; the review measured it (see below).
- **Only h1–6 is corrected,** from two band equations with their own label maturity. The newest training label is 4 to 9 months old, against 13 to 15 in R23. Older labels still fill most of the window.
- **Do no harm:** a correction is applied only when nested chronological validation beats applying none.

The declared conditions for carrying a candidate forward were: headline h6 at or below FAST on both samples; core h6 at or below FAST on both samples; a positive needed-against-applied correlation; no seasonally contaminated feature. **No candidate meets all four.**

- The labour-cost candidate improves full-sample core accuracy by 4% at h3 and 7% at h6, and its correction is now in phase with need: correlation +0.33, +0.42 and +0.43 at h3, h6 and h12, against −0.58 in R23. It fails from 2024: at h6 the correction averaged +0.14 log points when FAST needed +0.06, the two were barely correlated (0.15), and the circular bootstrap intervals for the 2024+ headline loss difference exclude zero on the wrong side at h3 and h6 (for core, at h6 only).
- Imported momentum is the only candidate at or below FAST on headline in both samples, but its core accuracy is worse in both (1.784 against 1.758; 0.430 against 0.394). Its headline gain is error cancellation against other blocks, which is exactly what the second condition exists to catch.
- The repaired level-gap control is the worst candidate from 2024, which supports the review's reading that level gaps were the wrong transformation.

My reading of the labour-cost reversal: the measure is growth of real unit labour cost over one to three years. In 2021–23 that growth coincided with thin margins and passed through. In 2024–25 the same growth was a catch-up from depressed real wages while margins were wide, and it did not pass through. A margin-level measure against a long norm would distinguish the two, but one Czech cycle cannot validate it. That belongs with parameters estimated outside this sample, not with another correction learned here.

A constant 2% forecast scores as many joint successes as any model (two from the August 2023 report, one from August 2025). Carrying the last annual rate forward scores twice as many: four from the February 2022 report and two from August 2024, the same report that gives R23's joint candidate its third success. The test as designed measures the regime, not skill. Episodes are not independent either: FAST's 19 come from 13 reports, losses from 9 and gains from 3.

## Verification

45 targeted tests pass (36 new, 16 of them added after the review). The run verifies FAST path equality before substitution; h0, every noncore value and every weight are preserved, and a test now asserts it on the delivered file.

**Independent review.** No blocker: the implementation matches the specification, there is no look-ahead, and every fit, path, score, gate and lead-test count was rebuilt independently. Its findings, all adopted here:

1. *The level term survives.* Predictors are scaled without centring, so a feature with a non-zero mean still carries a level term. In the 2019–2022 training windows 61–66% of the labour-cost gap's second moment is its mean. Split into coefficient times training mean and coefficient times deviation, the mean part of the applied h6 correction correlates −0.57 with need and the deviation part +0.47 (joint candidate: −0.59 and +0.33). R23's explicit intercept scored −0.58. So the repair of the delayed echo was partial. It rescues nothing: with the deviation part alone the labour-cost candidate's h6 core RMSE is 1.603 (delivered 1.629, FAST 1.758) on the full sample and 0.522 against FAST's 0.394 from 2024. The failure from 2024 comes from the labour-cost signal itself.
2. *A wrong sentence* about where the base-rate successes come from, corrected above, and four table cells that were 0.001 too high.
3. *Tests.* 19 of 37 behaviour-changing mutants survived the first suite (the do-no-harm rule at path construction, fold selection, tie rules, the estimator's objective, both attribution rules, publication gating of the seasonal-naive benchmark, the origin month in the base rates). Every surviving behaviour was correct on the delivered data. `tests/test_r23b_review_mutants.py` now pins each one with a hand-computed expectation, and re-running the reviewer's 37 mutants against the extended suite kills all 37 (`work/claude_mutation_recheck_20260917/recheck.py`).
4. *One gate tolerance.* The bound test uses 1e-10 and the bounded solver leaves up to 2.2e-10 on six coefficients. Two cells of `gate_coefficient_signs.csv` would move (3.3% to 6.7% and 61.1% to 64.4%); neither is quoted here and no selection depends on them.

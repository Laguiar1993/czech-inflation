# R25 results: panel-estimated mean reversion of the core trend does not pass

17 September 2026, Claude. **Four candidates, one declared run, none promoted.** Shrinking the FAST core trend toward a robust norm, with the speed estimated on an EU panel that excludes Czechia, helps origins in 2022–23 and from 2024 and fails the short-horizon and full-sample conditions. The specification named this outcome in advance as the clean negative for unconditional mean reversion. The nowcast and every noncore block are unchanged.

[Specification](docs/implementation/R25_PANEL_PERSISTENCE_SPEC_2026-09-17.md) (committed as `c7eb093` before any data were analysed; code as `3d34e59` before the run) · [Interactive CNB rounds](output/research_r25/final/evaluation/cnb_rounds_replayed_r25.html) · [Author's reconstruction checks](work/research_r25_review/output_checks.py)

## What was done

Eurostat's HICP excluding energy, food, alcohol and tobacco for the 26 member states other than Czechia, 1996 to 2025, is frozen under `data/research_r25/`. Every country is run through the unchanged R15 filter at 108 quarter-end origins with its own-origin seasonal pattern, exactly as Czech core is. For each three-month band, `lambda` is the pooled no-intercept slope of the realised deviation from the country's norm on the FAST forecast's deviation from it, clipped to [0, 1] and re-estimated at every Czech origin from bands already published (1,492 to 2,298 rows). The norm is the median of overlapping twelve-month changes. The Czech core path becomes `norm + lambda * (FAST - norm)` band by band. Only this one weight is borrowed from the panel.

- **My stated expectation was wrong on the profile.** I expected 0.8 to 0.95 for h1–3, falling with the horizon. The panel says 0.40 to 0.53 for h1–6 and 0.55 to 0.65 for h7–12. Month-to-month HICP core is noisy (package holidays, clothing), so less than half of a filtered short-run deviation survives.
- Persistence does rise with the distance from normal: at h10–12 the weight is 0.28 to 0.48 for deviations below the panel median and 0.57 to 0.66 above it.
- The Czech-only control is clipped at 1.0 until 2023 (Czech history says deviations grew) and is 0.56 to 0.60 afterwards.
- **The Czech norm is the weak point.** Core ran near zero from 2009 to 2016, so the own-history median was 0.8% a year in 2019 while core was running at 2.5 to 3.5%. It reaches 2.0% only in 2026.

- In 2019–21 shrinkage pulled a rising core path toward a 1% norm and made a bad under-prediction worse (core bias at h12 −4.6 against −3.1).
- In 2022–23 it halved the core error. The headline still got worse, because FAST's core over-prediction had been offsetting the administered-price under-prediction; with the core error gone the headline bias at h12 moves from +0.2 to −2.0. This is the error cancellation that block-first evaluation exists to expose.
- From 2024 it helps at every horizon, in phase with need (correlation 0.73 to 0.98), and the combination with the R24 food drift has the lowest h12 headline error of any path in that era (0.593). Under the declared rule that is a regime trade-off, and nothing is promoted.

## What this closes and what it leaves

R21's a-priori anchors, R23B's learned corrections and now a panel-estimated weight all fail the same way: a symmetric pull toward a norm hurts while inflation is building and helps while it unwinds, and the net depends on which phase dominates the sample. Taking the speed from outside the Czech sample did not change that. Two things are now clear.

1. **The norm matters more than the speed.** An own-history median was far too low for Czech core before the surge. A target-consistent norm was tried in R21 and also failed on the full sample.
2. **The missing information is which phase we are in.** That is a statement about upstream pressure building or fading, not about core's own history. The panel can be extended for that (producer and import prices by country exist at Eurostat), which is a larger, separately declared project. R23B found producer-price momentum to be the one imported-cost measure with a stable sign.

Kept from this round: the frozen panel and the machinery to run the unchanged filter on any country, which also serves the portability goal; the finding that short-run filtered deviations of HICP core are mostly noise; and the confirmation that persistence rises with distance from normal.

**Erratum.** The specification says R24 showed that "a robust long-history norm works for food". The R24 review showed less: a stable drift near 2.6% a year works in this cycle, and the estimator is not separately validated. The specification is a hash-frozen input of this run, so it is corrected here and not edited.

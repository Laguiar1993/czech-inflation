# R24 results: a stable food drift improves the path

17 September 2026, Claude. **Four candidates, one declared run. `FOOD_NORM_SHIFT_R24` meets all four declared conditions and replaces the baseline food path in the research roster.** An independent review confirmed every number and found no look-ahead. It also showed that the result is narrower than my specification's language: what works is a stable food drift of about 2.6% a year in place of a recent-window mean, in this one cycle. The estimator that produces that number is not separately validated. The independent nowcast, core and every other block are unchanged.

[Specification](docs/implementation/R24_FOOD_DRIFT_SPEC_2026-09-17.md) (committed as `a10939f` before any fit; code as `b74136d` before the run) · [Independent review](work/research_r24_review/REVIEW.md) · [Interactive CNB rounds](output/research_r24/final/evaluation/cnb_rounds_replayed_r24.html)

## Why food

Block attribution, now standard output, shows where the shared path's error sits for origins from 2024 (RMS of the cumulative weighted block error over h1–12, percentage points of headline): food 0.73, fuel 0.44, administered 0.32, core 0.32. R21 to R23B all worked on core. The food path's twelve-month forecast was no better than forecasting no change, and from 2024 it carried a bias of +2.3 log points.

The cause is in `models/food_stable_r14b.py`. The system centres monthly rates on calendar-month means of a window of at most 96 months, and its forecasts decay to that centre. The centre therefore carries the window's average drift, 4.4% to 5.4% a year once the window contains 2022. R24 keeps the seasonal shape and the system's decaying deviation and replaces only that drift.

## What the review changed in the interpretation

All four declared conditions hold for the three non-control candidates, and the declared preference order selects the simple shift. The reviewer's findings limit what that means.

1. **The rule cannot tell the long-history median from a constant.** The median is effectively a constant here (2.51–2.82, standard deviation 0.07). A plain 2.6% a year scores the same: 7.352 and 3.397 against 7.357 and 3.373. Every constant from 1.75 to 4.75 would have passed all four conditions, and the full-sample headline leg keeps rewarding ever higher drifts because it repairs the 2021 under-prediction.
2. **The start year is a tuning constant.** At the first origin the median is 2.25 starting in 1998 and 3.58 starting in 2010. My specification's phrase "needs no tuning constant" was wrong, and so is the sentence in the R25 specification that R24 showed "a robust long-history norm works". Both specifications are hash-frozen inputs of their runs, so they are corrected here and not edited.
3. **The gain is a level shift, not better dynamics.** The correlation of forecast with outcome from 2024 is unchanged at −0.59. Within that era the candidate is worse at all 8 origins from January to August 2024 and better at all 11 from September 2024; three origins (May to July 2025) carry 79% of the era's net gain.
4. **The full-sample gain is concentrated.** 58 of 75 origins improve. Origin years 2023 and 2021 contribute 44% and 31% of it; 2019, 2020 and 2024 together 6%. The loss difference has a lag-one autocorrelation of 0.78, so there are about six independent blocks; a t-test on non-overlapping block means is significant at 5% in 4 of 12 alignments.
5. **Five code mutants survived my first tests.** Five tests were added after the review (12 tests now pass), and re-running the reviewer's eight mutants against the extended suite kills all eight. The frozen outputs were right on all five points.

**What I would say at work:** the food path now returns to a stable long-run food inflation of about 2.6% a year, not to the average of the last eight years. That removed a bias of about 2 points in twelve-month food forecasts after the 2022 surge and improved the assembled path at h6, h9 and h12 in every era of the sample, leaving h3 unchanged. It does not forecast food turning points any better, and the evidence comes from one cycle.

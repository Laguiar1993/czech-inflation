# Czech CPI path models — R16 results and assessment

The damped-slope experiment improves annual headline path accuracy, but the attribution audit finds weaker core forecasts whose errors interact more favourably with errors elsewhere in the model. It also finds no demonstrated anticipation of fresh core-inflation turns. The gentler slope is the more balanced new **headline research challenger**; the aggressive slope is stronger over the full history and weaker recently. I would retain FAST/current core as the principal comparison lines rather than replacing them on these headline results alone.

## What was built

**A core trend with a slope.** The model splits monthly, seasonally adjusted log core inflation into a persistent level and a temporary component. Unlike R15 FAST, the persistent level has a slope: pressure can keep increasing or decreasing before its slope gradually fades. The temporary component decays separately. Forecast seasonality is then restored using the same saved origin-specific seasonal factors as R15.

The state is `(level, slope, temporary)`. Each monthly transition uses `level_next = level + phi*slope`, `slope_next = phi*slope`, and `temporary_next = 0.8*temporary`. Four prespecified settings cross slope persistence `phi=0.8/0.95` with slope process variance `0.001/0.01`; the latter controls how readily new observations move the estimated slope. Other filter settings are fixed in the specification. The .95/.001 variant is gentler; .95/.01 is more responsive. There is no clipping, survey anchor or adjustment to improve the reported results.

The .8 slope and temporary component have the same decay, so their adjusted forecast collapses to a monotonic path. The .95 versions can generate a reversal because the slope and temporary component decay differently. That mathematical capability does not guarantee useful turns in actual data.

An adaptive version selects one setting for the **whole twelve-month path**, using only previously issued paths whose twelve outcomes were already released. It compares the last 36 common eligible origins and requires 24; it cannot choose the setting that later works best at the forecast being scored. It selected .8/.001 on 73 of 90 outer origins, .8/.01 on 10, and .95/.001 on seven. It never selected .95/.01. The selection target is monthly log-core accuracy, not eventual annual-headline accuracy.

**Two separately forecast price signals.** A second approach first predicts broad annual services and goods inflation, then asks whether those predictions help forecast core residuals relative to the saved R15 FAST path. Inputs are:

| First-stage model | Predictors |
|---|---|
| Services | Its current annual log inflation rate; changes over 3 and 12 months; unemployment change; unit-labour-cost growth; seasonally adjusted industrial-production growth |
| Goods | Its current annual log inflation rate; changes over 3 and 12 months; import prices; total industrial and manufacturing producer prices; FX change; industrial-metals prices |

These broad ARAD annual NSA series include first-round tax effects. They are **signals**, not an exact monthly partition of CNB core CPI. Dividing a projected annual log change by twelve normalizes predictor units; it does not recover an observed monthly inflation rate or an official basket weight.

Each first-stage regression predicts the change from the latest observed annual signal to its average over h1–3, h4–6, h7–9 or h10–12. Separate second-stage regressions predict the corresponding average monthly core residual. Four versions use core acceleration alone, acceleration plus projected services, plus projected goods, or both signals. Training uses the forecasts actually saved at each historical origin, never the future goods/services outcomes in place of those forecasts.

Both stages use ridge regression, up to 120 released training origins, a minimum of 48, training-only imputation/scaling, and three prespecified penalty values selected chronologically. The constant is explicitly penalized. Missing required first-stage forecasts exclude a second-stage row. The final correction is constant within each three-month band; this is a small forecasting system, not a continuous structural model of pass-through.

A fixed half blend combines the adaptive slope and both-signal transmission in monthly log-core units. These ten candidates were compared with five frozen controls, on **90 origins from February 2019 to July 2026**, h0–h12. Full evaluation uses the same common support as R15; no easier sample was substituted.

## Annual headline accuracy

RMSE below is in percentage points of annual inflation. Full-history h3/h6/h12 have 84/81/75 common observations; origins from 2024 onward have 28/25/19. Longer-horizon observations overlap and the recent sample is small.

| Model | Full h3 | Full h6 | Full h12 | Recent-origin h3 | Recent-origin h6 | Recent-origin h12 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Independent bridge | 1.537 | 2.516 | 5.395 | 0.569 | 0.678 | 1.347 |
| Stable pipeline | 1.532 | 2.525 | 5.418 | 0.544 | 0.660 | 1.210 |
| Current core | 1.537 | 2.624 | 5.397 | **0.472** | **0.467** | **0.708** |
| R15 FAST | 1.459 | 2.290 | 4.869 | 0.518 | 0.536 | 0.858 |
| **Gentler slope: .95/.001** | 1.439 | 2.239 | 4.810 | 0.514 | 0.516 | 0.716 |
| **Aggressive slope: .95/.01** | **1.405** | **2.002** | **4.464** | 0.568 | 0.681 | 1.248 |
| Adaptive slope | 1.477 | 2.396 | 5.092 | 0.502 | 0.525 | 0.950 |
| Both-signal transmission | 1.531 | 2.525 | 5.222 | 0.520 | 0.540 | 0.957 |
| Adaptive/transmission half blend | 1.502 | 2.449 | 5.147 | 0.510 | 0.528 | 0.937 |

The gentler slope improves on FAST at all six displayed horizon/sample combinations, with recent h12 close to current core. The aggressive slope improves full-history h6 RMSE by about 13% relative to FAST, but its recent h6 and h12 performance deteriorates. Its full-history gain is therefore not a reason to deploy it as the sole path model.

The target-month frame tells a different story: at h12 for targets from January 2024 onward, aggressive slope RMSE is 1.487, pipeline 1.686, FAST 1.788, gentle slope 1.862 and current core 2.619, each on 31 observations. That frame includes forecasts issued before 2024. A model can perform well during the transition into a period and less well when forecasting from inside it; neither frame should replace the other after seeing the scores.

There is also a component-level qualification. Full-history h12 cumulative log-core RMSE is 4.196 for FAST, 4.569 for the gentle slope and 4.945 for the aggressive slope, on 78 core observations. Their headline gains do not establish better core forecasting. The same-support accounting diagnostic below confirms that this difference survives the headline sample and measures its interaction with retained-block errors.

Twelve-origin moving-block intervals for both .95 slopes' full-sample squared-loss improvements over FAST span zero at h3, h6 and h12. These are descriptive dependent-sample intervals, not an untouched holdout or an adjustment for all the models tried across research rounds.

## A headline gain can coexist with a weaker core forecast

After the primary results, a separate [accounting diagnostic](output/research_r16/attribution/definitions.json) replaced every h1–h forecast of monthly core with its realised value, retaining the same core weight and every other model contribution. This uses future information **only to attribute historical errors**. It is not a usable forecast, a new candidate or an input to selection. No forecast was altered in the scored experiment.

The resulting counterfactual has the same retained error for all twelve compatible models. Working in annual-log units gives an exact identity: `total error = core replacement effect + retained error`. The retained error includes noncore/accounting blocks and, for h below twelve, unchanged h0. At h12 the annual window excludes h0.

On the exact **75 original common headline dates**:

| Model | Cumulative-core RMSE, log pp | Annual-headline RMSE, log pp |
|---|---:|---:|
| FAST | **4.278** | 4.451 |
| Gentle slope | 4.659 | 4.398 |
| Aggressive slope | 5.038 | **4.101** |

These headline numbers use log units to preserve additive accounting, so they differ from the ordinary annual-CPI RMSEs above. Squaring the identity shows exactly what changes relative to FAST. For the gentle slope, mean total squared error falls **0.466**, despite a **1.050 increase** in the core-effect squared term, because the error-interaction term improves **1.517**. For the aggressive slope the corresponding changes are **−2.987 = +2.203 − 5.191**. The retained-block squared term is unchanged.

The interaction is the uncentered cross moment `2*mean(core_effect*retained_error)`, not necessarily covariance. The gentle model reduces positive alignment between errors; the aggressive model's full-h12 cross moment becomes negative, indicating offsetting errors. The gentler model's recent h12 headline improvement also coexists with worse same-date cumulative-core RMSE: 1.157 versus FAST 0.586, on 19 dates.

The aggregate improvements remain real measurements for this fixed model system. They do **not** establish a better core mechanism, and the ranking may change when the noncore models or their biases change. That is why I would keep the slope as a research comparison while prioritizing component-consistent improvement. See [paired attribution results](output/research_r16/attribution/paired_vs_fast_summary.csv); the algebra reconciles within 6.4e−14 squared log units.

## Why the extra signals did not help

The weakness is already visible before translating the signals into core. On each signal's own identical forecast/persistence sample, the full-history RMSEs are:

| Signal and forecast | h1–3 | h4–6 | h7–9 | h10–12 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Goods: learned projection | **1.245** | 3.559 | 5.149 | 6.185 |
| Goods: persist current annual signal | 1.603 | **3.117** | **4.493** | **5.674** |
| Services: learned projection | 1.277 | 2.619 | 3.756 | 4.317 |
| Services: persist current annual signal | **1.243** | **2.361** | **3.384** | **4.256** |

Units here are annual log percentage points, not monthly core or headline RMSE. Samples have 87/84/81/78 observations. For recent origins, services persistence is particularly strong: h10–12 RMSE is 0.247 versus 1.178 for the learned projection. Goods also loses to persistence at that recent long band, 0.869 versus 0.538.

This does not prove that wages, imports, services or metals have no economic information. It shows that this particular target, input set, lag design and selection procedure did not extract a reliable improvement. Annual rates mix underlying pressure with base effects; a small regression can extrapolate a past episode into a period where persistence is more accurate. The second stage then adds another estimated mapping and another source of error. More predictors alone do not repair a weak intermediate forecast.

The [source-contribution report](output/research_r16/interpretation/source_contributions.csv) traces each second-stage correction back through its first-stage inputs, including centering and penalized constants. All 1,440 fitted corrections reconstruct within 2.8e−16 monthly log percentage points. The contributions explain the fitted arithmetic; they are not causal effects or CPI weights.

## CNB comparisons and material deviations

The two clocks use the latest saved model snapshot before the CNB report date, and through the CNB information-cutoff date, respectively. Both retain 66 common report/quarter pairs from 18 scoreable reports and 18 distinct realised quarters. These are dependent comparisons. The final, nineteenth report remains visible even where outcomes cannot yet be scored.

| Model, report clock | Quarterly MAE | Quarterly RMSE | Material gains / losses versus CNB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CNB | **1.061** | 2.295 | — |
| R15 FAST | 1.260 | 1.987 | 15 / 32 |
| Gentler slope | 1.246 | 1.907 | 13 / 29 |
| Aggressive slope | 1.115 | **1.679** | 13 / 29 |

A material gain is at least **0.15pp less absolute error** than CNB; a loss is at least 0.15pp more. A directionally correct but tiny improvement does not count, and an excessive overshoot can lose. The aggressive model's better RMSE is meaningful as an aggregate error result, but 13 material gains against 29 losses is not a broad or frequent edge.

At the cutoff clock, aggressive-slope MAE/RMSE is 1.223/1.765, with 11 material gains and 35 losses. Removing each CNB report in turn exposes the same concentration as R15: both .95 slopes keep their RMSE advantage under 17 of 18 omissions, but lose when **10 February 2022** is omitted. On the remaining 62 report-clock comparisons, CNB RMSE is 1.476, gentle slope 1.691, aggressive slope 1.623 and FAST 1.739. The main sample is never changed; this is a concentration diagnostic.

For reports from 2024 onward, on 34 common pairs, CNB report-clock MAE/RMSE is 0.287/0.372, current core 0.275/0.402, FAST 0.346/0.473, gentle slope 0.362/0.480 and aggressive slope 0.518/0.727. The small MAE advantage of current core is worth monitoring, not a demonstrated trading edge.

The February 2022 result shows that projecting more inflation than CNB helped on some large subsequent misses. It does not establish that the model anticipated the war. Judging economic plausibility requires examining assumptions and reactions to new information as well as realised errors. Proximity to CNB is a benchmark, not the objective used to fit these independent models.

## Direction, underlying turns and revisions

At h12, the gentler slope correctly calls the direction of 46 of 66 material headline moves, versus FAST 43 and bridge 37. The aggressive slope gets 44. This is annual inflation relative to the latest known annual rate, with a 0.25pp materiality threshold; known base effects can generate that direction without a new turn in underlying pressure.

The separate core-turn diagnostic removes each origin's saved seasonal pattern and compares four three-month annualized core bands. An internal peak or trough requires opposite adjacent changes of at least 0.5pp. There are 159 common opportunities, including 77 realised turns. All slope variants predict **zero material internal turns**. The two-signal transmission predicts 29, with zero exact-band hits and 29 false calls. Goods-only gets two hits from 25 calls; services-only one from 20. On recent origins, no new candidate has an exact hit.

Thus R16 removes one architectural restriction for the .95 slopes but has not demonstrated the desired advance-turn skill under this predeclared test. Nor is this a comparison with CNB core-turn forecasts, which are absent. Quarterly aggregation, exact-band timing and the fixed threshold define the test; they should travel with the counts rather than being silently relaxed after disappointing results.

Matched-target revisions have mean absolute size 0.539pp for current core, 0.637 for gentle slope, 0.646 for FAST and 0.782 for aggressive slope, on 1,035 common consecutive-origin/target pairs. Future targets remain included even without realised outcomes. The more aggressive line is less stable; revision magnitude alone does not tell whether a change was justified by news.

## What to use and what to improve next

Keep the practical roster small:

| Role | Model and decision |
|---|---|
| Independent next release | Existing HARD_BASE and its established HALF/FULL comparisons; unchanged |
| Reproducible path reference | Existing bridge/stable pipeline |
| Simple responsive path benchmark | R15 FAST; retain it because better headline scores in R16 do not imply a better core mechanism |
| Recent-period comparison | Current core |
| New research challenger | Gentle slope .95/.001, shown beside FAST/current core; no automatic promotion |
| Sensitivity line | Aggressive slope .95/.01; its historical responsiveness comes with recent errors and larger revisions |
| Retained negative experiments | Adaptive slope, signal-transmission variants and fixed half blend; no silent averaging into the main path |

My next priority would be **component-consistent forecasting**, before a larger search over parameters. The attribution above supplies the reason: the new slope has not improved its own core target. For an interpretable component system, improve or explicitly model the retained-block errors rather than attributing the favourable aggregate interaction to better economic transmission. Error compensation can improve a fixed aggregate forecast; it is not necessarily portable to a changed component system.

For a new services/goods experiment, use available monthly series with a documented relationship to the CNB core definition, preserve historical basket changes, and first test each intermediate projection against literal persistence and a small autoregression. Any shrinkage toward persistence or revised target must be declared as a new experiment; it was not quietly inserted into R16. Annual broad signals can remain predictors without being described as monthly components.

A later trend experiment could let measured forecast innovations determine adaptation speed, with a rule frozen and selected chronologically. Selecting the aggressive setting for crises and the gentle setting for calm periods after observing those periods is not such a rule. R16's adaptive selector illustrates why choosing a winner from past errors can lag changing conditions and why the target used for selection matters.

Energy, FX and policy assumptions should also have explicit scenario paths and recorded news revisions. That makes disagreement with CNB interpretable without requiring the baseline to guess an unforeseeable event. No war period was removed from the primary scores. Policy start/expiry accounting, exposure weights and recorded input vintages remain priorities for the next-release product; they were held fixed in this experiment.

# CZK Cpi Forecasting - Latest models

R13 research assessment, 9 September 2026. This round tests BASE and Categories
with their own half/full error corrections, four core-path specifications and a
separate labour pair. It also examines the user's distinction between a forecast
miss caused by a difficult common environment and a weak economic model.
No operating forecast is replaced. No survey, expectations or retailer data
enter the new estimators. Original numerical models and results are preserved.

## The user's point changes how the path should be assessed

A sensible forecast can be very wrong after an unforeseen shock. A point forecast
is not a promise that war, oil, fiscal policy or tariffs will follow its implicit
or explicit assumptions. Large realised errors alone do not prove an unsound model.

However, proximity to CNB is supporting evidence, not proof of economic validity.
Two forecasts can share mistakes, and CNB can have a different information set.
We should judge realised accuracy, the explanation of the original forecast,
and its subsequent updates separately. This round adds that separation instead
of deleting crisis years from the main scoreboards.

## Nowcast: the full family comparison now exists

Half and Full mean 50% and 100% of an error adjustment learned from that family's
own past raw core forecasts, multiplied by the forecast origin's core weight.
The category correction does not borrow BASE errors. It initially corrects the
combined category core forecast; it does not estimate six additional forests.
The old `TARGET_OWN_HALF` remains a BASE/category blend and is a different object.

### Why matched-history controls were necessary

Category levels start in 2015. After the unchanged 48-observation raw-model warmup,
the first feasible category error is February 2019. The forest needs 40 released
errors, so both new matched families apply zero correction for the first 40 scored
releases. Active corrections start June 2022: 50 releases and 13 of the 23 big events.
The ten earlier large surprises cannot test a correction that had no training data.

BASE also received matched controls using precisely those error dates and decision
clocks. Their all 90 RMSE is 0.41420 Half and 0.41222 Full. Category Raw/Half/Full
remain 0.40895/0.40944/0.41098 on those same90 dates. On the active 50, category
RMSE worsens 0.45925 to 0.46250 from Raw toFull, while big-event MAE improves
0.53192 to 0.51907 on 13 events. This does not reverse the practical verdict.

Existing BASE correction history was originally generated at 09:00 on release eve,
whereas saved scored forecasts and the new matched histories use 23:59. We kept
the existing comparators and rebuilt matched histories at the actual saved clocks.
The distinction is documented; empirically the overlapping raw BASE errors differ
by zero in this sample. Neither clock nor history was silently rewritten.

## Path: retain the bridge, but keep the component evidence

Four fixed alternatives compare aggregate versus five-category core, crossed
with monthly versus cumulative targets. They use own lags, destination-month
seasonality, conservatively lagged FX and import prices, and fixed ridge shrinkage.
Only future core changes; h0, noncore blocks and destination weights stay fixed.

The cumulative target is an arithmetic sum of component monthly rates, allowing
the statistical remainder to recombine exactly. It is not a compounded component
price index. Final headline annual inflation always compounds monthly gross
changes exactly. Arithmetic versus compounded core differences are also reported.

Recent three-month category forecasts improve modestly, but the four variants

persistence. The separate unemployment addition also loses to its matched control:
recent h12 RMSE 3.023 versus 2.396; bridge 1.347 on those same 19 origins. Labour's
shorter archived history is excluded from the primary four-model coverage rule.
Wages were not invented or reconstructed using the known future-dependent method.

### A better core component can coexist with a worse headline forecast

The full matched h12 category-monthly model has lower cumulative core error than
the bridge. To distinguish this from headline error cancellation, a separate
post-fit diagnostic replaces future core with realised core while retaining the
same noncore forecasts, weights and h0. This perfect-future-core calculation is
evaluation only: it is not a forecast, a new scored model or causal shock attribution.

For any candidate, its annual error equals its deviation from this common
replacement path plus the replacement path's error against actual headline CPI.
The squared-error identity then measures changed core error and changed offsets.
On 54 full h12 outcomes, category monthly lowers the annual core-error RMSE from
3.682 to 2.938pp. Its headline MSE gain is nevertheless **-4.045pp²**, decomposed as
**+4.927pp²** from better core forecasting and **-8.972pp²** from lost favourable
offsets against other errors. RMSEs themselves cannot be added.

This justifies preserving the category-core mechanism as a research candidate,
especially while noncore mechanisms are improved. It does not justify promoting
the entire path. In the recent 19-origin panel, annual core error also worsens
(0.844 to 0.995pp), so the recent problem cannot be explained solely by offsets.
We should not tune a model to reproduce accidental historical cancellation.

## CNB comparison: shared misses and divergence are both present

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2022 | 16 | 4.202 | 4.615 |
| 2023 | 16 | 2.150 | 0.375 |
| 2024 | 16 | 0.560 | 0.370 |
| 2025 | 15 | 0.661 | 0.383 |
| 2026, available outcomes | 3 | 0.303 | 0.319 |

These are quarterly-average annual inflation errors across forecast horizons,
not twelve-month endpoint errors. All 66 scored pairs represent 18 unique quarters.
The 2022 results support the importance of a difficult common forecasting
environment. The 2023 divergence deserves separate investigation rather than
being attributed automatically to the original war shock.

Across 66 pairs, both errors have the same direction on 44. Sixteen pairs are large
shared misses (both absolute errors>=0.5pp in the same direction). The bridge is closer
on 20 and CNB on 46. These counts do not identify how many shocks were unpredictable.
The exact difference between our forecast and CNB is not a pure specification
error; CNB's realised error is not a pure external shock.

Information timing matters. Our matched model paths precede report publication by
a median 8 days, up to 30 days; the CNB's internal cutoff differs again. For example,
the February 2023 comparison for 2023Q1 is model 11.36%, CNB 16.31%, actual 16.39%, but
the model path was frozen 29 days earlier. That is a clear gap to investigate,
not proof that all of it came from model structure. Missing/intermediate forecasts
and not-yet-realised outcomes remain in the coverage and revision tables.

The CNB's own review of forecasts made in 2022 discusses mistakes in foreign,
fiscal and administered-price assumptions. Its review of the 2024 forecasts also
asks whether errors originated in assumptions or model behaviour. This supports
the distinction raised by the user. [CNB 2022 review](https://www.cnb.cz/en/monetary-policy/monetary-policy-reports/boxes-and-articles/Assessment-of-the-fulfilment-of-the-2022-forecasts),
[CNB 2024 review](https://www.cnb.cz/en/monetary-policy/monetary-policy-reports/boxes-and-articles/Assessment-of-the-fulfilment-of-the-2024-forecasts/).

## What to use and what remains worth building

Keep BASE Raw as the operating independent reference, existing BASE Half/Full as
correction comparisons, and Category Raw as the research accuracy challenger.
Category Half/Full and matched-history BASE variants remain named research outputs.
The independent bridge remains the main path reference; no new whole path wins.
Retain category-monthly core as component research with its recent weakness visible.

The next meaningful infrastructure is a dated assumptions and component archive:
what was assumed about FX, commodity prices, administered bills and policy at each
forecast; which inputs changed at the next run; and why the path moved. Recompute
model paths at explicitly comparable CNB cutoffs where snapshots permit. Without
such records, subtracting CNB from our forecast cannot isolate economic mistakes.

Economic sensitivities and uncertainty should be evaluated separately from point
accuracy. A forecast could be close to CNB for the wrong reasons. Conversely, an
explained deviation based on information or a different conditional assumption
can be economically sensible. Future conditional scenarios must stay separate
from the independent central forecast and from honest historical accuracy tables.

This round has not tuned penalties after seeing results, restored survey inputs,
certified probability bands, built a trading rule or integrated category models
into the live refresh loop. Frozen-input reproducibility and live certification
are different requirements. Historical values retain the declared latest-vintage
and reconstructed-availability limitations; none of these reused windows is a
new holdout. A prospective archive remains essential.

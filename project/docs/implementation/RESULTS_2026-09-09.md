# CZK Cpi Forecasting - Latest models

## Decision

Keep an independent short-term component baseline, with the HALF and FULL
past-error adjustments shown alongside it. Exclude professional and household
inflation expectations from that independent family. ESI was tested separately
and did not justify inclusion. Preserve the expectations-conditioned model as
an optional comparison, without blending it into the independent point.

The work improves correctness, reproducibility and the clarity of the model
roster. It does **not** establish an excellent standalone trading signal or
unconditional superiority over consensus. A relatively small improvement in
forecasting accuracy can be useful, but it needs to survive new releases.

## Parameter estimation and optimization

The existing models already estimate parameters. In core and food, ridge
regression re-estimates the coefficients using the available historical slice.
The penalty is fixed at 3. In the residual forest, trees learn nonlinear
relationships and quantile-combination weights are optimized on a recent
validation window. Tree complexity, feature sampling, validation-window length,
recency weighting and correction size are separate design choices. The path
models use numerical maximum-likelihood estimation, alongside imposed structural
assumptions and restrictions.

Therefore it is reasonable to investigate more extensive tuning. The distinction
is between fitting coefficients and choosing the settings that govern that fit.
More computation does not guarantee a stronger future forecast. It can also find
an unusually successful explanation of the crises already in this sample.

I ran two new, explicitly bounded searches. At each outer forecast date, the
choice uses only earlier forecast errors whose outcomes had been published. The
next release is evaluated after that choice. This is the time-series version
of separating [model selection from its evaluation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/model_selection/plot_nested_cross_validation_iris.html);
random train/test splits would violate the required [time order](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit.html).

**Core ridge search:** four penalties, 0.3/3/30/300, crossed with 60-month,
120-month and expanding histories. It produced 2,244 sequential fits. Selection
used the latest 36 eligible core errors, with at least 24 required. Overall
headline RMSE worsened from 0.417952 to 0.419449 and MAE from 0.262516 to 0.270661.
The recent 2024+ RMSE improved, but the full sample and flash era did not. The
fixed default stays.

**Residual-forest search:** four forests crossed minimum leaf sizes 3/8 with all
features/one-third feature sampling. All used 200 trees, seed 42 and twelve
chronological validation errors. A second prior-error selection chose the forest
and a correction multiplier of zero, half or one. It generated 544 origin/config
records, including 384 fitted forecasts and explicit insufficient-history rows.

The tuned strategy's headline RMSE was **0.413013**, about **1.18% better** than
BASE; MAE was 0.262233, almost unchanged. Its 2024+ RMSE worsened from 0.218978 to
0.221033, while flash-era RMSE improved from 0.173002 to 0.165500. Its large-surprise
MAE was 0.474699, with 8 material wins and 1 material loss. Its selected alerts
still accumulated a negative absolute-error advantage against consensus,
−0.697695 pp. This is mixed research evidence, not grounds for a new trade rule.

These searches were computationally manageable on this computer. There is no
evidence here that a huge search would improve the model dramatically. The
better next use of computation is carefully controlled testing of economically
different information and stable specifications, rather than making the grid
arbitrarily large. Every historical comparison remains exposed to the broader
project's repeated model selection; nesting does not erase that history.

## Independent release nowcast: measured results

All numbers below use the same 90 first-release outcomes, February 2019–July
2026, and their matching surveys. The target is the regular release before the
flash era and the flash release from 2025. The suspect August 2026 survey is
excluded. Units are percentage points of monthly CPI inflation.

| Forecast | All-release RMSE | All-release MAE | 2024+ RMSE | Flash-era RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original BASE | 0.419917 | 0.270011 | 0.216598 | 0.169696 |
| Independent BASE | 0.417952 | 0.262516 | 0.218978 | 0.173002 |
| Independent HALF | 0.413696 | 0.264373 | 0.219588 | 0.165601 |
| Independent FULL | 0.413931 | 0.269627 | 0.225317 | 0.163827 |
| Independent BASE plus ESI | 0.421634 | 0.267533 | 0.219375 | 0.173354 |
| Nested forest selection | 0.413013 | 0.262233 | 0.221033 | 0.165500 |

The baseline without expectations is essentially as accurate as the original
baseline. ESI worsens overall RMSE and fails the predeclared practical promotion
rule. HALF has the best RMSE among the fixed independent trio, but BASE has
slightly lower MAE and slightly lower recent RMSE. Paired block-bootstrap
intervals for HALF/FULL's RMSE advantage over BASE include zero. These small
differences support retaining a simple reference and clearly labelled challengers.

The independent FULL and HALF error histories were rebuilt from their own
sequential ridge forecasts. They never read the legacy past-error file. An
independent poisoning check changed every historical professional/household
expectation to huge artificial values: all six hard/sentiment forecast points
were unchanged, to numerical precision.

## Your surprise-capture objective

A correct direction with a negligible deviation is not the objective. We measure
the reduction in absolute error relative to consensus and count a material win
only when the reduction reaches 0.15 pp. A material loss uses the symmetric
threshold. Overshoots are penalized through the actual forecast error; a capture
ratio by itself is only a diagnostic.

| Forecast | MAE on 23 big surprises | Material wins / losses | Correct direction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Independent BASE | 0.484583 | 7 / 3 | 17 / 23 |
| Independent HALF | 0.479371 | 7 / 1 | 16 / 23 |
| Independent FULL | 0.476061 | 9 / 2 | 17 / 23 |
| Consensus | 0.578261 | — | — |

“Big” means an actual first-release surprise of at least 0.4 pp. FULL reduces
average absolute error on those events by approximately 17.7%. That is material
conditional performance, not merely a 0.0001 pp improvement.

But an alert must be defined before the outcome. With an alert at a forecast
deviation of at least 0.2 pp, BASE/HALF/FULL each flags only **7 of the 23** big
surprises. They produce **13/15/16 false alarms**, respectively, where the actual
surprise is below 0.4 pp. FULL has 23 alerts, and its accumulated absolute-error
advantage on them is **−1.290482 pp**. An alert can be a useful small-surprise
forecast and still be a false alarm for the specific big-surprise definition;
the error-gain scoreboard accounts for that distinction.

The tuned forest flags 8 of 23 big surprises and has 15 false alarms. It reduces
some losses, but does not solve the selection problem. No alert statistic here
is a rates-market P&L, and overlapping economic shocks are not independent draws.
There is only one large surprise in the 19-release flash-era comparison.

## Repairs and safeguards implemented

- Incomplete prospective announcement rows now fail closed instead of falling
  through into the legacy reconstructed block-unit mapping.
- Independent input policies propagate into each policy's error history and
  forest, with publication-gated training outcomes.
- State-space transition timing, target changes, known lag propagation and
  BVAR horizon alignment were corrected. The BVAR prior is invariant to a
  predictor's units. Gap-model filtering and simulation now agree.
- Numerical estimation retries and fallbacks are explicit. Missing inputs are
  treated consistently; infinities are rejected and nonfinite projections do
  not masquerade as successful forecasts.
- Quarterly comparison includes known months and requires three finite monthly
  YoY values. Incomplete final quarters are omitted.
- The new runner uses one decision clock, archives the exact frames and reads
  them back before calculating. It records unrounded points, block contributions,
  runtime/binary identity, input/source hashes and completion time.
- Readiness checks include component histories and weekly fuel, not only the
  headline edge. Historical fixture calls can never become prospective evidence.
- Offline replay rejects changed, missing or nonfinite points and changed input,
  source or runtime identities. Result receipts protect the saved outputs too.

The extra month in the original path implementation arose from combining an
explicit data shift with the state transition's own timing. The corrected
implementation is consistent with the [state-space convention](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/statespace.html)
and now has deterministic impulse tests for that boundary. Passing those tests
establishes numerical correctness of that mechanism, not forecasting superiority.

## Path models and the economic interpretation

The path comparison uses 90 common origin dates for the primary experiment and
133 older origins for no-driver controls. Each model starts from exactly the same
independent h0. Every intermediate monthly rate is retained and compounded into
YoY; the h12 YoY window no longer contains h0. Conditional-h0 calculations remain
separate and use the current stored/index-derived CPI outcome, not a reconstructed
historical first-print series.

The new unemployment archive contains **103 actual publication vintages**,
January 2018–July 2026. Each origin selects the full history then published,
including the revisions known at that time. This removes the major mistake of
using a 2026 unemployment vintage in a 2019 information set. FX still uses a
completed-month availability assumption and current stored observations. Other
cached inputs do not suddenly become genuine historical vintages.

Among the newly fitted aggregate models, the restrained direct forest beats the
target-only trend at horizons three, six and twelve. On the primary common
rows its YoY RMSE is 1.742/2.767/5.494 versus 1.924/3.322/6.470 for target-only.
The BVAR is best at h1 among that new roster. Adding unemployment and FX to the
anchored trend does not improve its overall accuracy. These economically
plausible channels are not automatically useful forecasts in this implementation.

The stored component reference is stronger than these new aggregate challengers
on their narrower common sample. A fresh independent component-path test and
matching CNB quarter comparison are recorded in `BRIDGE_RESULTS.md`. They remove
expectations at every horizon, rather than merely changing the initial h0.

The fresh independent bridge is the preferred starting path. Its overall YoY
RMSE at h1/h3/h6/h12 is **0.911/1.537/2.516/5.395**. On exactly those same rows,
the independent forest scores 0.975/1.772/2.817/5.602 and the seasonal naive
1.040/2.123/3.633/6.572. Sample sizes are 88/84/81/75. The smaller early food
samples cannot support every direct forecast; these missing forecasts remain
visible, with no invented replacement to improve the scoreboard.

For targets in 2024 onward, the bridge scores 0.365/0.547/0.748/1.892 on 31
targets per horizon. It is especially stronger than the aggregate alternatives
at six and twelve months. The forest is slightly stronger in the crisis h12
panel, so there remains a useful reason to monitor both. These retrospective
results justify a manageable research roster, not a claim of an undisputed winner.

The CNB comparison uses the latest generated path whose information cutoff
precedes the report date, with complete quarterly averages. Across 66 matching
report-quarter pairs, bridge RMSE is **2.362**, versus **2.295** for the CNB and
2.433 for the stored F1b reference. From 2024 reports, the bridge is **0.590**,
versus **0.372** for the CNB, 0.555 for stored F1b and 0.450 for the optional
stored survey-conditioned trend. Thus the independent path still trails the
CNB in the recent calm period. The 66 pairs contain only 18 distinct target
quarters and 18 reports, so they must not be treated as 66 independent tests.

A structural interpretation remains useful: current prices and known tariffs
should dominate the near horizon; persistence, demand/slack, exchange-rate
pass-through and monetary policy matter over longer horizons. The statistical
evidence does not support selecting a model merely because its coefficients have
appealing signs. The target-anchored trend missed the inflation surge badly.
The [CNB forest research](https://www.cnb.cz/export/sites/cnb/en/economic-research/.galleries/research_publications/cnb_wp/cnbwp_2026_09.pdf)
motivates nonlinear challengers, but its targets, features and evaluation are not
identical to this project's compounded twelve-month path.

Keep an independent component path and a small independent challenger roster;
show the CNB forecast and optional survey-conditioned reference beside them.
Agreement with the CNB is a useful plausibility check, not the selection loss.
Disagreement should be traced to blocks, starting data and future-driver
assumptions. It should not be manufactured to create a more interesting trade.

## Energy and uncertainty: where the larger opportunity lies

The new ledger separates commodity charges, distribution/fixed charges, VAT,
monetary credits, caps, start dates and expiries. An expiring credit does not
automatically imply that a separate levy waiver also expires. Incremental event
contributions explicitly replace the energy contribution already embedded in the
baseline, avoiding double counting.

The test scenarios reveal how much household exposure matters. Plausible
assumption combinations generate very wide effects; some literal allocations
even imply a negative proxy bill and are rejected. These are sensitivity
envelopes, not probabilities or fitted historical forecasts. Customer contract
shares, representative consumption, actual bill components and CPI allocation
must be documented before this ledger supplies a production point.

The November/December prospective entry means recording the published policy,
price schedule and mapping at that time, with a timestamp before January's
outcome. A git date alone is not externally immutable proof, but an archived
source-and-forecast record written before the release is substantially stronger
than a row reconstructed with knowledge of the answer.

Simple sequential error bands also remain imperfect: nominal 90% bands cover
87.9% of the eligible BASE/HALF outcomes and 86.4% of FULL outcomes. They use
only previously published errors, but serial dependence, changing regimes and
selection mean they are not guaranteed or prospectively calibrated intervals.

## What I would prioritize next

1. Run and archive the independent family at fixed checkpoints and after material
   data releases. Record both the decision cutoff and actual completion time.
2. Improve source-level prices and energy/CPI exposure mapping. Better tuning
   cannot recover information absent from the inputs.
3. Preserve observation vintages for the remaining revised series and improve
   the treatment of missing releases. Extend the path's direct models to exploit
   newly known market data at their genuinely available date; their current
   fixed lag inputs do not use every observation the ML scenario path uses.
4. Validate the component path, forecast distributions and alert selection on
   new data. Use joint block errors if constructing contribution uncertainty;
   summing independently sampled component risks would discard their dependence.
5. Only then evaluate instrument-specific rates reactions, entry timing and
   costs. Inflation-error gains alone do not establish a rates-trading return.

The accompanying operating guide explains the commands and archived files. The
portable package retains the supplied research separately from these changes,
including source evidence, test results, release-level scoreboards and both
tuning selection schedules. The original checkout was left unchanged.

## Verification and delivery

All **230 tests passed**, with three retained X-13 diagnostic warnings. A full
frozen example reproduced its nine nowcast points and 91 monthly path points
offline. The nowcast matched the independently scored 90-origin table within
1.2e-16 pp; its contributions summed exactly. The historical example is labelled
retrospective and cannot qualify as a forecast made before the outcome.

All 454 files checked in the original supplied checkout remained byte-identical.
The independent branch preserves the supplied unfinished research in an earlier
checkpoint, followed by the reviewed implementation. The portable ZIP contains
the code, data snapshots, historical source archive, results and Git history;
it requires the documented Python environment and external X-13 executable.
New live forecasts additionally require the refreshed local economic database.

# CZK Cpi Forecasting - Latest models

Independent review, 9 September 2026. Nowcast correctness, survey independence, and the new inflation-path work.

## My assessment

The component nowcast is a worthwhile research model with a sensible economic structure. Its current historical forecasts reproduce. It is competitive with consensus recently, but it has not established reliable superiority over consensus across the full evaluation period, or a dependable rule for identifying profitable large deviations beforehand.

The path work is substantially less mature. I found reproducible timing errors, a historical-vintage problem, and a live CNB-comparison error. These affect the interpretation of the new experiments. Correct them before using the results to choose additional variables, infer economic relationships, or introduce a changing credibility parameter.

I recommend an independent main forecast. Keep CPI-release consensus, FMIE analyst forecasts, and CNB projections as external comparisons. A separately labelled survey-conditioned forecast can remain useful. The evidence does not establish that your own path forecast must consume other people's inflation forecasts.

Production code and model settings were not changed during this review.

## 2. What the nowcast results actually say

All numbers below are percentage points of **monthly CPI inflation**, scored against the first release: regular CPI before the flash era, flash thereafter, using the corresponding stored consensus. The suspect August 2026 survey row is excluded. These are release-eve forecasts, not forecasts necessarily available several weeks earlier.

The common sample contains 90 releases, February 2019–July 2026. The short component sample remains a limitation despite much longer headline CPI history.

### Ordinary forecast accuracy

RMSE penalises large mistakes more heavily. Lower is better.

| Forecast | All 90 | Ex-January, 83 | 2024+, 31 | Flash-era 2025+, 19 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BASE | 0.420 | 0.404 | 0.217 | 0.170 |
| FULL | 0.421 | 0.399 | 0.230 | 0.167 |
| HALF | 0.415 | 0.396 | 0.220 | 0.165 |
| BASE without forecast/sentiment surveys | 0.418 | 0.403 | 0.219 | 0.173 |
| Release consensus | **0.382** | **0.380** | 0.241 | 0.195 |

HALF deserves its accuracy-challenger role: it has the lowest full-window RMSE of the three original models. BASE is slightly better in the 2024+ window. None is an unambiguous winner. The recent gains over consensus are encouraging but come from 31 observations, including only 19 flash-era observations. The full-window survey advantage survives removing January.

Removing the five survey-related core features changes headline forecasts by approximately 0.031 pp on average in absolute value, with a maximum change near 0.158 pp. Therefore the survey inputs do affect individual forecasts, even though aggregate RMSE changes little.

For no-survey minus BASE RMSE, the full-sample difference is -0.00197 pp. A paired moving-block bootstrap gives a descriptive 95% interval of approximately [-0.0099, +0.0049] using six-month blocks. In 2024+, the difference is +0.00238 pp, with interval [-0.0016, +0.0062]. Three- and twelve-month block results also straddle zero. These intervals do not adjust for all previous model searches. They are stronger support for “no clear accuracy difference detected” than the specification's unsupported universal 0.005 “noise band.”

### Meaningfully capturing large surprises

Use the existing definitions: a large realised surprise is at least **0.4 pp** from consensus; a material win/loss improves/worsens absolute error by at least **0.15 pp**. There are 23 large surprises.

| Forecast | MAE on 23 large surprises | Right side of consensus | Material wins / losses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BASE | 0.506 | 15/23 | 7 / 1 |
| FULL | **0.479** | **17/23** | **10 / 3** |
| HALF | 0.490 | 16/23 | 8 / 1 |
| No-survey BASE | 0.485 | 17/23 | 7 / 3 |
| Consensus | 0.578 | — | — |

FULL cuts average absolute error by 0.099 pp, about **17% relative to consensus**, on these selected events. That is meaningful. But 10 material wins out of 23 events is different from “10 wins versus 3 losses”: the latter leaves ten events that were neither material wins nor material losses. Only one large surprise is in the 19-release flash-era subset, so the evidence for catching large **flash** surprises remains particularly thin.

The benefit is not entirely dependent on reconstructed January events: on the 19 large surprises outside January, FULL has MAE 0.486 versus consensus 0.595, with nine material wins and two material losses. The signal deserves further testing even while the energy mechanism is repaired.

The practical problem is selection before the print. On the 32 occasions when FULL itself lies at least 0.2 pp from consensus, it is closer to the print on 17 occasions, yet its aggregate absolute-error improvement is **-0.725 pp**, or **-0.0227 pp per alert**. Wrong deviations cost more than the right ones save under that rule. This is a forecast-error calculation, not a trading-return backtest.

Therefore: the model contains useful information during some shocks; the current evidence does not establish a profitable or reliably beneficial alarm rule.

### The scoring rule I would use

Let actual = A, consensus = C, forecast = F. Record:

`absolute-error gain = |A-C| - |A-F|`.

This directly rewards being meaningfully closer. On large surprises, also show signed capture `(F-C)/(A-C)` and normalised accuracy gain `1 - |1-capture|`. Exact capture scores 1; a forecast at twice the surprise scores 0 because it is as far from the print as consensus. Overshooting should not get a free pass until 2×. Report aggregate gain divided by aggregate absolute surprise as a robust companion to individual capture ratios.

Maintain separate all-release accuracy, realised-large-surprise, and forecast-triggered-alert panels. Freeze thresholds before new outcomes. Do not optimise only on the retrospective large-surprise subset, which cannot be identified at decision time.

## 3. Surveys: what is currently independent, and what is not

The active nowcast does **not** use the consensus for the imminent CPI release as a regressor. It **does** use `exp12`, `exp36`, `household_exp`, `esi` and `exp12_x_state`. The first two are professional inflation expectations; the others include expectations/sentiment surveys. FMIE is a recurring analyst survey of future inflation and other economic variables, distinct from the next-release consensus. [CNB description](https://www.cnb.cz/en/financial-markets/inflation-expectations-ft/).

There is nothing intrinsically dishonest about forecasting with information from a public survey. It changes the product's meaning: the forecast partly incorporates other people's expectations. Given your preference, an independent main version is a reasonable design choice, especially because the nowcast ablation shows little aggregate accuracy cost.

Current dependency gaps:

1. The live no-survey column removes the five features only from BASE. FULL and HALF are still the original models.
2. Dropping features from a future forest call is insufficient. `cz_struct.py:379` loads `data/exact_historical_core_errors.csv`, generated by the original survey-inclusive base. A survey-free residual forest needs its **own complete sequential error history**, features, standardisation, fit and calibration pools.
3. `path_live.py:189` anchors every path at survey-inclusive BASE. Line 230 uses that same h0 even for the line labelled survey-free. The anchor affects year-on-year forecasts through h11; it drops out of the twelve-month window at h12. F1b additionally uses survey-inclusive core features at future horizons.
4. The no-survey column has no corresponding fully evaluated uncertainty product. Do not borrow the original model's bands and present them as independently calibrated.

Here “survey-free” should mean no professional forecast or sentiment-survey inputs. It should not accidentally ban official observed statistics such as CPI or Labour Force Survey employment simply because their statistical collection uses surveys.

Recommended output: independent main forecast, independently constructed challenger, and separately labelled external comparisons. Keep an optional survey-conditioned path for information. Never average it into the independent line silently. Agreement with CNB is a diagnostic, not an objective to optimise.

## 4. Corrections needed

### A. Path drivers enter one month later than the stated equations — confirmed

Files: `models/trend_gap.py:130,136,175,196`; `models/gap_model.py:134,171,116`.

Statsmodels defines the transition as `state[t+1] = T[t] state[t] + intercept[t]`. The code shifts drivers once and then places them in this transition intercept. As a result, the state at t receives the original driver at t-2, although the stated equation says t-1. [Statsmodels equation](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/statespace.html).

Controlled test: put a unit pulse in the third input month, fix its coefficient to one and persistence to zero, and use missing observations to expose the pure transition. The declared effect is month four; the actual effect is month five.

The new gap model compounds this problem: its simulation explicitly uses the intended unemployment and FX lags, while estimation has an extra lag. The fitted and simulated equations are different. The changing target's transition intercept has the same index-convention issue around target changes.

Correction: define every driver by reference month and availability time, construct transitions with the library's convention, and use one transition implementation for filtering, simulation and handover. Acceptance tests must cover an impulse, a target change, and equality between a filter projection and a manual zero-shock simulation. Re-estimate affected path models and withdraw coefficient-sign interpretations until then.

### B. The path loader uses a 2026 unemployment vintage historically — confirmed

File: `path_step2_backtest.py:45`, inherited by later driver loaders.

The database query ignores `release_date`, `fetched_at` and source vintage. Read-only inspection found the January 2008, January 2019 and January 2024 unemployment rows all stamped **release_date 2026-08-31**, retrieved from the July 2026 release. The table has a single value per month/adjustment/series/gender, not multiple vintages. Applying `.shift(1)` does not reverse revisions or smoothing.

This is especially consequential because CZSO introduced the new published trend-cycle presentation in June 2025 and described a changed adjustment model estimated using data from 2015 onward. Applying that current history to earlier forecast origins is not evidence of historically available information. [CZSO methodology announcement](https://statistikaamy.csu.gov.cz/jak-ocistit-nezamestnanost).

Correction: recover release-vintage tables where possible; otherwise use a clearly labelled latest-vintage experiment and an alternative built from released observations with one-sided transformations. Retain the vintage limitation for other revised macro inputs too. Do not solve this by throwing away all old observations whose *current file* was published in 2026: archive retrieval or an honestly labelled alternative is needed. Measure the effect; this audit has not measured it.

### C. The trend forecast discards known driver information at the forecast boundary — confirmed

`models/trend_gap.py:175–196` projects the filtered gap only geometrically. Its documentation permits zero future change-type drivers, which is a legitimate conditional assumption. But known lagged drivers crossing the forecast boundary must still enter before the unknown future starts.

With fixed coefficients, changing the last observed driver by one leaves all tested forecasts unchanged. For the stated lag-one model, the first forecast should change by its coefficient. The recent driver is shifted away, and the manual projection never restores it. Known lagged real-rate, FX and import-price information must propagate through a calendar-aligned scenario path; setting *unknown* future changes to zero is a different operation.

### D. BVAR benchmark horizon and scale problems — confirmed

Files: `models/bvar.py:85,99–100,112`; caller `path_step2_backtest.py:96`.

Training pairs lagged values at t-1 with the target at t+h, but prediction supplies the last observed value T to that lag-one slot. The implied target is T+h+1. The caller already converts its desired horizon to h+1, so the extra month remains.

Controlled example: a series increasing exactly 1% each month, with effectively unpenalised one-lag regression. Asking for h=1 returns **3.3333908**, the T+2 value, rather than **3.3003869**, the T+1 value.

The Minnesota-style penalty also uses the target/source variance ratio where its documented prior requires deriving the inverse precision consistently with the likelihood. Rescaling an otherwise identical predictor by 100 changes the forecast from -0.11926 to -0.13373 in the test. This is avoidable dependence on measurement units, not new economic information.

Correct horizon alignment, derive the penalty from the declared prior/likelihood, and test predictor- and target-unit transformations. Then rerun F3. It is already an explicitly simplified direct regression; its label should not imply a full joint structural BVAR.

### E. Incomplete announcement rows can fall back to legacy units — confirmed safeguard failure

File: `cz_struct.py:739–787`.

The dated-row gate and current economic-unit conversion work on the existing data. However, if a prospective/documented row has one missing item change and a non-missing old `announced_regulated_mm_est_pct`, the code accepts that legacy value. My test uses a future row with missing gas change: `_gate_value(..., announce_mode='documented')` returns **16.5** instead of rejecting the incomplete economic entry.

No existing scored row was shown to fail this way. It is a live-input safeguard defect. Restrict legacy fallback explicitly to the reconstructed-scenario mode/provenance. Missing item data must produce a validation failure or an explicitly approved assumption, never an automatic unit switch. Test `_gate_fires` and `_gate_value` together.

### F. The live CNB comparison uses incomplete quarters — confirmed

File: `path_live.py:256–279`.

The quarterly table averages only forecast rows and then compares them with full-quarter CNB forecasts without requiring three months. In the stored September run, 2026Q3 is September alone, **2.1759%**. Including July and August from the frozen history/explicit flash assumption gives approximately **1.9583%**. Against CNB's stored 1.9045%, the apparent gap shrinks from about **+0.271 pp to +0.054 pp**.

Join released months with forecast months to build complete quarterly averages. Do not compare a partial final quarter either. Keep counts as a check, not as a substitute for matching the forecast object. This affects the live display; I did not find the same incomplete-quarter construction in the saved report-date comparison harness.

### G. Operational consistency and unsuccessful fits need explicit treatment

- `path_live.py` separately loads frames and calls `struct_live`; the row masking and within-month FX replacement performed inside `struct_live` do not propagate to the path's h1–h12 frames. Unify the decision timestamp and frozen input object. The code inspection establishes a consistency gap; this review has not demonstrated a particular contaminated historical h1–h12 value from it.
- Non-converged path fits are still returned and scored. Stored parameter files contain two E4, one E5, three E6 and one E7 failures, plus failures in step five. Use a predeclared retry/fallback rule and report its frequency. Do not quietly delete difficult origins to improve scores.
- The path displays the old step-one engine's RMSE scale alongside F1b. This is disclosed in the text but is still easy to misread. Attach error distributions to the exact engine, forecast stage and horizon.
- Keep monthly indexes complete. Positional target shifts in ridge/BVAR and `.dropna()` compression can silently change the horizon if an interior month is missing. Add an explicit calendar-gap test.
- A run needs an input cutoff, completion time, data/parameter/source hashes, unrounded outputs, and immutable evidence. An append-style CSV update is not by itself an immutable archive or protection against concurrent writers. Validate offline replay on the second computer before calling the live system certified.

## 5. The administered-price mechanism: better, still not settled

R8 improved the design materially: calendar publication gating, call-specific published basket weights, and economic item changes instead of a stored fitted-weight conversion. The January 2023 entry is unavailable before 11 January 2023 because it uses the December index; the calendar respects that date. It cannot support a claim of having that final magnitude ready in November or December.

Three remaining economic issues matter more than adding another statistical model:

**January 2022 source interpretation.** The current row calls 52.3% electricity and 30% gas the pre-change regulated shares. ERU's 1 December 2021 text describes electricity's unregulated share as 47.7% at the previous year-end and 59.4% “today”; gas's regulated share moves from 30% to 18.5%. That text does not establish the old shares as December 2021 shares immediately before January repricing. The page also carries a later update date. Archive and reconcile the original bill/product tables and the precise denominator. Separately verify whether each supplier percentage describes the commodity component or the total bill. [ERU source](https://eru.gov.cz/regulovane-slozky-cen-energii-neprekroci-inflaci).

**January 2023 units.** Removing a fixed monetary credit should be represented additively in the household bill level, while a percentage tax/discount acts multiplicatively. I agree with that economic principle. I do not regard one average Eurostat electricity price as a proven conversion into the relevant September CPI credit weight. Contract types, fixed charges, consumption and the CPI sample still matter. The additive correction is an improvement, not exact item-level replication.

**Gross change versus incremental change.** A seasonal administered-price baseline already embeds normal energy repricing. Adding the full energy contribution risks counting that part twice. Model energy subitems and the remaining administered basket separately, or subtract the baseline energy contribution before adding the event contribution. The solved administered coefficient is an empirical reconciliation coefficient, not a literal official economic share.

For the next declared challenger, I favour all-month event accounting over a 1.1 pp activation threshold. Record policy start and expiry as separate events, price both under the same household exposure model, and propagate a price level through time. Use exposure uncertainty to moderate effects. This is a proposed new specification; it should not retroactively replace the frozen scored version without a new label and full comparison.

A prospective November entry should freeze the information and assumptions actually known then. Later announcements or newly released indexes can update it in new timestamped versions. One should not promise a final January magnitude before its inputs exist. Each version must remain recoverable.

These are future **year-on-year inflation** errors; they are not comparable with the approximately 0.4 pp monthly nowcast RMSE. F1b has a shorter eligible component window. On its 66 common h12 origins, its RMSE is 5.788 versus naive 6.990, a 17.2% reduction. Comparing its 5.788 with F2's 3.839 from another sample would be misleading.

At CNB report dates, F1b and F2 share the same 66 report-quarter rows:

|---|---:|---:|---:|

The component bridge was competitive in the crisis. CNB was better recently, including versus the survey-conditioned line. Repeated forecasts of the same quarters are not independent observations. The h0-print object used here is a rebased historical reconstruction, not evidence that those paths were published live then; same-day release/report timestamps also need exact ordering.

I would revise several prose claims:

- “The path needs surveys” is too strong. Conditional models can help, but the comparison is neither a general theorem nor an untouched prospective result. E5's similar stored RMSE is not a reason to crown E5 either; its implementation must first be repaired.
- Correct coefficient signs do not establish causality or identify policy transmission. A negative wage coefficient does not show wages cannot cause inflation. Feedback, correlated predictors, measurement errors and inappropriate lags can produce such signs.
- “The failure is economic, not statistical” and “the CNB architecture transfers” go beyond the evidence, especially with the transition mismatch. The proposed IS equation is a small reduced-form approximation, not an identified CNB transmission model.
- Repeated amendments after inspecting the same 2008–2026 history make that history development data. Declaring the next variant before another run is good discipline, but it does not make already inspected outcomes untouched.
- E7 should not be the default independent line merely because it narrowly won one old selection slice. Keep it in research pending repair; do not automatically substitute the retrospectively best-looking alternative.

## 7. The structure I recommend

Maintain two forecasting products sharing one audited data and release system.

### Next CPI release

Retain core, food, fuel, alcohol/tobacco, administered prices and the explicit reconciliation term. This is easier to diagnose than one large headline model and suits the mixed economic drivers of monthly CPI.

Prepare an independent BASE candidate as the main view. Rebuild survey-free HALF and FULL from their own sequential residual history, then freeze their roles: HALF as an accuracy challenger, FULL as a stronger-deviation challenger. Do not infer position size from FULL's magnitude alone. Keep the existing trio as historical references while the independent candidates accumulate prospective evidence.

Priority improvements: exact energy/event accounting; a clock-matched live pipeline; complete survey removal; uncertainty calibration; error attribution separating goods, services/housing, food and policy shocks. The frozen core already contains lagged services CPI, so “add services CPI” would not be a new idea. Splitting goods, services and housing is a different, more demanding experiment and needs non-overlapping targets and valid weights.

### Twelve-month inflation path

Start with the independent nowcast and a short component bridge. Treat roughly the first three months and the later path as different forecasting problems; test the handover horizon rather than assuming three or six months is optimal.

For months four to twelve, begin with a repaired, small independent trend-and-gap baseline. Prefer modelling persistent core/services dynamics while carrying energy, food and tax effects separately. Allow the long-run inflation target to anchor the trend softly. A public 2% policy objective is not another analyst's forecast, and it should not mechanically force the forecast to 2% at month twelve.

Add one structured challenger with a small number of economic channels and a specified future path for each driver. Unknown future FX can follow a declared flat-level assumption; that still implies evolving year-on-year FX changes as old base months roll out. Known past import, wage or interest-rate movements must retain their delayed effects. Interest-rate scenarios require an explicit distinction between a statistical forecast and an identified policy counterfactual.

Produce internally consistent monthly price-index paths, then compute monthly YoY, quarterly averages, and annual averages from them. Carry joint forecast errors through the path: twelve individual 90% intervals are not a 90% interval for the entire trajectory.

### A small active roster

| Independent FULL | Surprise challenger; evaluate false alarms as well as conditional wins |

Archive the other experiments with their specification and verdict. One registry should record the target, horizon, data cutoff, input dependency set, version, role and evidence status. F1b/E7/S_M codes can remain internal identifiers; the user-facing labels should explain what the forecast is.

## 8. What to take from the papers, and which data to explore

The local **CNB WP 9/2026** is relevant: it studies quantile forests, dynamic combinations of quantiles, inflation components, and an external sector suited to a small open economy. The useful next experiment is a restrained nonlinear challenger using the same audited inputs and forecast clock as the linear model. Learn any combination weights only from outcomes already released at that origin. Its approximately 0.66 headline RMSE figures refer to future monthly inflation targets, not your twelve-month YoY path. The paper also discusses difficulty extrapolating into inflation regimes absent from the training history. Its existence does not certify this project's residual forest or its probability bands. [CNB paper](https://www.cnb.cz/export/sites/cnb/en/economic-research/.galleries/research_publications/cnb_wp/cnbwp_2026_09.pdf).

The **g3+ framework** offers a more important architectural lesson than a particular algorithm: external prices, exchange rates, domestic costs/activity and expectations have connected transmission mechanisms, and near-term information feeds a broader projection system. Your monthly unemployment regression is not equivalent to that framework. I would borrow its separation of economic channels and explicit assumptions without attempting to build a miniature DSGE immediately. [CNB framework description](https://www.cnb.cz/en/monetary-policy/inflation-reports/boxes-and-annexes-contained-in-inflation-reports/Introducing-the-g3-extended-projection-model/), [2024 update](https://www.cnb.cz/en/monetary-policy/monetary-policy-reports/boxes-and-articles/The-updated-g3-core-forecasting-model-and-the-shadow-forecast/).

The local **Blockwise Boosted Inflation** paper suggests grouping predictors by economic channel and applying carefully chosen shape restrictions. That can make a challenger easier to interpret and constrain than unrestricted feature proliferation. It remains UK evidence, and restrictions do not magically identify causal effects in Czech data. The existing `bbim_lite` result should not be presented as an exact replication of that paper. [Bank of England paper](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2025/blockwise-boosted-inflation-non-linear-determinants-of-inflation-using-machine-learning).

The nonlinear dimension-reduction paper in your folder is useful later if the verified predictor panel becomes genuinely large. It is not my first priority for a short, repeatedly searched Czech release sample.

Candidate data, in priority order:

| Channel | Useful candidate | Why / necessary qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Household energy and tax | Product-level electricity/gas price lists, distribution tariffs, fixed fees, policy start/expiry, exposure shares | Directly addresses the largest accounting weakness; price lists alone do not reveal all household contract exposures |
| Foreign cost pressure | Czech import prices and German/euro-area non-energy versus energy producer prices, mapped through EUR/CZK | Particularly relevant to tradable goods and the later path; avoid double-counting FX already embedded in CZK import prices |
| Domestic persistence | Services and goods detail; rents/imputed-rent-related series; scheduled excise changes | Distinguishes sticky inflation from volatile inputs; avoid overlapping CPI aggregates |
| Domestic demand and labour | Retail volumes, employment/hours, vacancies with definition-break checks, wages and productivity together | Prefer a few released indicators over one heavily smoothed unemployment gap; quarterly wages must respect actual release timing |
| Food pipeline | Existing farmgate and food-manufacturing PPI; targeted retail/produce observations only with a new reason | The current pipeline already has supply-chain stages; closed SZIF/category experiments should not be reopened merely because another feed exists |
| Fuel | Existing pump observations with exact publication times; short-term oil/FX information for unobserved weeks | Exploit timeliness and coverage before changing the algorithm; retain the documented aggregation approximation |

ARAD is useful for stable access to monetary/financial data and selected CZSO/MLSA economic series. It is not proof that every desired subseries or its historical publication vintage is available there. For each proposed input, verify the exact definition, continuity, original release time, revisions and value actually available at the forecast cutoff before testing it. [CNB economic-statistics coverage](https://www.cnb.cz/en/statistics/general-economic-stat/).

1. Preserve the current branch/output evidence. Fix the incomplete-announcement fallback and partial-quarter comparison, with targeted acceptance tests.
2. Repair transition timing, known-lag propagation and the BVAR horizon/penalty. Add deterministic tests before rerunning affected historical models. Correct unsuccessful-fit handling.
3. Separate vintage-aware and latest-vintage path experiments. The unemployment example provides a concrete starting point. Recompute the scoreboards on common rows and exact release/report times.
4. Build a fully independent nowcast family, including new sequential error files and matching calibration histories. Freeze the existing survey-inclusive comparisons. Complete one-clock live inputs and offline replay on the destination computer.
5. Freeze the main scorecard: general accuracy; material large-surprise capture; forecast-triggered alerts including false alarms; calibrated distributions. Current V1 bands have only about 82–83% coverage for nominal 90% over the scored history, and none met the project's selection condition. Treat them as uncalibrated estimates, not verified trading probabilities.
6. Build the all-month, item-level energy/event challenger and a small independent path baseline. Only then spend the next experimental slot on a nonlinear path challenger or a new economic data channel.

For each new experiment specify the economic hypothesis, input availability, fixed comparison models, common origin set, selection window and acceptance rule first. Keep a permanent register of all trials. Use future releases as prospective evidence; do not repeatedly relabel the inspected historical window as untouched.

The immediate investment should be better accounting, correct time alignment and a trustworthy independent live forecast. There is enough useful signal here to justify continuing. The current evidence supports a serious forecasting research system, not a claim that an excellent trading model has already been established.
